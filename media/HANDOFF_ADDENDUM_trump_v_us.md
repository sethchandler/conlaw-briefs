## Case

Trump v. United States, 603 U.S. 593 (2024), decided July 1, 2024, 6–3, opinion by Chief Justice Roberts. Justice Barrett joined all but Part III-C. Sotomayor and Jackson dissented.

It arose from the federal case in Washington, D.C.: Special Counsel Jack Smith's four-count indictment of August 1, 2023, over the effort to overturn the 2020 election. The counts were 18 U.S.C. § 371, § 1512(k), § 1512(c)(2), and § 241. Judge Chutkan dismissed the case on November 25, 2024, after the election.

Seth wants the opening to place this case among the other criminal cases against Trump: New York (hush money), Florida (classified documents), and Georgia (state election racketeering).

## Seth's direction

- **Theme:** a Venn diagram of the immunity tiers, in which almost nothing is left in the "prosecutable" zone; the Ellipse speech on the border of Brandenburg incitement; how history has been rewritten; the mob and lawlessness.
- **Content:** include Trump's own words before January 6 and during it. "It is almost as if people have forgotten."
- **Balance:** he agreed to state the majority's rationale fairly before the dissents.
- **Style ideas offered (none chosen):**
  - a constitutional blueprint, with Venn diagrams that draw themselves;
  - a torn-paper collage;
  - a woodcut or linocut broadside, with the mob as one carved mass.
- **Music:** he chose "Distorted Americana," option 2, "Bugle in the Fog."
  - An original bugle call is echoed by out-of-tune "ghost" bugles.
  - A timpani heartbeat speeds up.
  - A clashing wordless choir stands in for the crowd.
  - The two-minute version, at 80 bpm with 6-second sections, runs: lone call to 0:18, a build to 1:00, the mob to 1:30, near-silence to 1:42, then the lone bugle sagging and fading.
  - Rejected: option 1, a fife playing Yankee Doodle that comes apart; option 3, a broken brass march ending in silence and a piano chord.
