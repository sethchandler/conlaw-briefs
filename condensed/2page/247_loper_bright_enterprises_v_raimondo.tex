{\LARGE \textit{Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo}, 603 U.S. 369 (2024)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
\textit{Chevron} is overruled: under the APA, courts must independently decide whether an agency acted within its statutory authority rather than defer to its reading of an ambiguous statute (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-451}{Cornell LII}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
A 2020 federal fisheries rule required Atlantic herring fishermen to pay for onboard observers, at up to \$710 a day. The Magnuson-Stevens Act expressly allowed industry-funded observers for three other groups but was silent on herring.

\section{Procedural History}
The D.C. and First Circuits upheld the rule under \textit{Chevron}; certiorari was limited to whether \textit{Chevron} should be overruled.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Vacated and remanded, 6--3 (6--2 in \textit{Loper Bright}, Jackson recused). Roberts for the Court; Thomas and Gorsuch concurring; Kagan, with Sotomayor and Jackson, dissenting.

\section{Holding}
Courts must exercise independent judgment on questions of statutory authority. Agencies may receive \textit{Skidmore} respect, express delegations of discretion are honored, and prior holdings upholding specific agency actions remain protected by stare decisis.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Roberts:} APA \S~706 directs courts to ``decide all relevant questions of law,'' and the judicial tradition since \textit{Marbury} never deferred on law. Ambiguity is not delegation; statutes have a ``single, best meaning.'' \textit{Chevron} proved unworkable and unsettling. \textbf{Thomas:} \textit{Chevron} also violated the separation of powers. \textbf{Gorsuch:} the Court ``places a tombstone on \textit{Chevron}.'' \textbf{Kagan:} expert agencies, not judges, should fill technical gaps; the Court chose ``a rule of judicial hubris.''

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} Whether a chemical used since 1950 is an ``emerging'' compound is decided \textit{de novo}. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} EPA's choice of a numerical threshold under a ``reasonable and necessary'' delegation receives deference. \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} An agency's reversal of a rule once upheld under \textit{Chevron} raises unresolved stare decisis questions.

\section{Critique}
Critics say the ``single best meaning'' is often a fiction that shifts policy choices to judges, and that fights have merely moved to what counts as a delegation.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``\textit{Chevron} is overruled. Courts must exercise their independent judgment in deciding whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority, as the APA requires. Careful attention to the judgment of the Executive Branch may help inform that inquiry. And when a particular statute delegates authority to an agency consistent with constitutional limits, courts must respect the delegation, while ensuring that the agency acts within it. But courts need not and under the APA may not defer to an agency interpretation of the law simply because a statute is ambiguous.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Chief Justice Roberts, 603 U.S. at 412--413.
