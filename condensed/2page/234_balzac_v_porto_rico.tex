\begin{center}
{\LARGE \textit{Balzac v. Porto Rico}, 258 U.S. 298 (1922)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
Citizenship did not incorporate Puerto Rico; in an unincorporated territory only fundamental rights apply of their own force, and the jury right is not one, because locality controls (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/258/298/}{Justia}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
After the 1917 Jones Act made Puerto Ricans U.S. citizens, editor Jesus M. Balzac published two 1918 articles attacking the Governor and was charged with misdemeanor criminal libel, for which local law allowed no jury.

\section{Procedural History}
Tried without a jury over his Sixth Amendment demand, Balzac was convicted and jailed; the Supreme Court of Porto Rico affirmed.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Affirmed, 9--0. Chief Justice Taft for eight Justices; Holmes concurred in the result.

\section{Holding}
The Jones Act did not incorporate Porto Rico. Only fundamental rights apply in unincorporated territories; jury trial is not one; and locality, not citizenship, governs. The First Amendment defense also failed.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Taft:} Justice White's \textit{Downes} framework is ``the settled law of the court.'' Incorporation requires an express declaration or an equivalent implication; the Jones Act's bill of rights pointedly omitted juries. Citizenship gave diplomatic protection and mainland rights, not juries on the island. Unlike Alaska, Porto Rico was distant and rooted in civil law, so residents could decide when to adopt the jury. \textbf{Holmes:} concurred without opinion.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} A mainland-born citizen tried in Porto Rico has no jury right there. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} Fundamental rights like Fourth Amendment protection apply even in unincorporated territories (\textit{Torres}). \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} A felony bench trial in a territory today tests whether \textit{Balzac} survives \textit{Duncan v. Louisiana}.

\section{Critique}
The Constitution nowhere distinguishes incorporated territories, as Justice Gorsuch noted, and \textit{Duncan} later held the jury right fundamental, stranding \textit{Balzac}'s premise.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``It is locality that is determinative of the application of the Constitution, in such matters as judicial procedure, and not the status of the people who live in it.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Chief Justice Taft, 258 U.S. at 309.
