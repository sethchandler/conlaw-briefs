\begin{center}
{\LARGE \textit{Trump v. Slaughter}, 609 U.S. 422 (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
\textit{Humphrey's Executor} is overruled: FTC Commissioners, who exercise executive power, must be removable by the President at will (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/25-332/}{Justia}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
FTC Commissioners may by statute be removed only for ``inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office.'' In March 2025 President Trump fired Commissioner Rebecca Kelly Slaughter without cause, invoking his ``authority under Article II of the Constitution.''

\section{Procedural History}
The district court reinstated Slaughter under \textit{Humphrey's}; the D.C. Circuit denied a stay; the Supreme Court stayed the injunction and granted certiorari before judgment.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Reversed 6--3. Roberts wrote for Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and (except Part III--B) Thomas; Gorsuch concurred; Sotomayor dissented, joined by Kagan and Jackson.

\section{Holding}
Article II gives the President unrestricted power to remove principal officers who exercise executive power. The FTC's protections are invalid, and \textit{Humphrey's} is overruled; non-executive bodies, the Federal Reserve, and non-Article III judges were reserved.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} the Framers chose a unitary, accountable President; \textit{Humphrey's} rested on an ``almost fictional view'' of the FTC, later precedent eroded it, and Congress cannot claim power by ``adverse possession.'' \textbf{Gorsuch:} delegated powers have now shifted to the President, so the Court should revive nondelegation and related limits. \textbf{Sotomayor:} the decision discards 90 years of precedent and a century of bipartisan practice in a ``profound bait and switch.''

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} NLRB and FERC members become removable at will. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} Federal Reserve Governors remain protected (\textit{Trump v. Cook}). \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} removing a Tax Court judge, a question Part III--B reserved.

\section{Critique}
Even sympathetic scholars warn of ``severability stacking'' that hands the President a windfall of delegated power; critics fault the history and the treatment of precedent and predict purges at each transition.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``When an agency `executes' a congressional mandate against private parties, it exercises executive power---no ifs, ands, or quasis about it.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Chief Justice Roberts, for the Court.
