\begin{center}
{\LARGE \textit{Murphy v.\ National Collegiate Athletic Association}, 584 U.S.\ 453 (2018)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
Congress may not forbid States to repeal their own laws any more than it may order them to enact laws; PASPA fell entirely (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/584/16-476/}{Justia}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
PASPA (1992) made it unlawful for States to ``authorize'' or ``license'' sports gambling, with exceptions for four States. In 2014 New Jersey repealed its bans on sports wagering at Atlantic City casinos and racetracks. The NCAA and the major professional leagues sued to enjoin the law.

\section{Procedural History}
The district court enjoined the 2014 Act, and the en banc Third Circuit affirmed 9--3, reasoning that PASPA required no affirmative state action. The Supreme Court reversed.

\section{Judicial Votes}
7--2 on commandeering and 6--3 on severability. Alito wrote for Roberts, Kennedy, Thomas, Kagan, and Gorsuch, with Breyer joining all but severability. Thomas concurred; Ginsburg (with Sotomayor and in part Breyer) dissented.

\section{Holding}
Congress cannot issue direct orders to state legislatures, affirmative or negative. Preemption must regulate private actors. PASPA's anti-authorization provision commandeers the States, and the rest of PASPA is not severable.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} the commandeering rule protects liberty, accountability, and state budgets, and the line between commands and prohibitions is ``empty.'' \textbf{Thomas:} severability doctrine rests on hypothetical intent. \textbf{Breyer:} the ban on private schemes should survive. \textbf{Ginsburg:} the Court used ``an ax'' instead of a scalpel.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a federal ban on States repealing cannabis penalties. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} a federal crime of operating a private sportsbook. \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} a federal drone ``right to operate'' that bars state zoning laws with little federal regulation behind it.

\section{Critique}
Federalists praised the Court for closing a loophole; critics say anti-commandeering lacks textual grounding and that invalidating all of PASPA was a policy choice. Progressive jurisdictions soon invoked \textit{Murphy} for sanctuary policies.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``The basic principle --- that Congress cannot issue direct orders to state legislatures --- applies in either event.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Justice Alito, for the Court.
