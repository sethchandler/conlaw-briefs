{\LARGE \textit{U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton}, 514 U.S. 779 (1995)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
States may not add term limits for Congress, even disguised as ballot-access rules; the Qualifications Clauses are exclusive (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/779/}{514 U.S. 779}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
Arkansas's Amendment 73 kept anyone elected to three House terms or two Senate terms off the general-election ballot, leaving only a write-in campaign. Its preamble condemned ``[e]ntrenched incumbency.''

\section{Procedural History}
Arkansas voters and the League of Women Voters sued; the state courts held \S~3 unconstitutional, the Arkansas Supreme Court affirming 5--2.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Affirmed 5--4. Stevens wrote for Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Kennedy concurred; Thomas, with Rehnquist, O'Connor, and Scalia, dissented.

\section{Holding}
The Qualifications Clauses are fixed and exclusive. States have no reserved power to add qualifications and cannot use the Elections Clause to handicap a class of candidates; change requires Article V.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} The Tenth Amendment reserves only pre-existing powers, and Congress did not exist before 1787; history shows the Framers barred state interference. \S~3 had the ``sole purpose of creating additional qualifications indirectly.'' \textbf{Kennedy:} dual sovereignty forbids a State from interposing between citizens and the National Government. \textbf{Thomas:} where the Constitution is silent, the States and their people retain power; the Clauses set floors, not ceilings.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a two-year district-residency rule for House candidates is invalid. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} term limits on a State's own officials are valid. \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} an accurate ballot notation of terms served, adopted by term-limit advocates.

\section{Critique}
The case turns on whether one national people or the separate peoples of the States made the Constitution. And States cannot offset incumbents' self-granted advantages, while Congress will not limit itself.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``Federalism was our Nation's own discovery. The Framers split the atom of sovereignty. It was the genius of their idea that our citizens would have two political capacities, one state and one federal, each protected from incursion by the other.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Justice Kennedy, concurring, 514 U.S. at 838.
