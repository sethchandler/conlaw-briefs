\begin{center}
{\LARGE \textit{Massachusetts v.\ EPA}, 549 U.S.\ 497 (2007)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
A State, given ``special solicitude,'' had standing to challenge EPA's refusal to regulate vehicle greenhouse gases, which are ``air pollutants'' under the Clean Air Act (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/549/497/}{Justia}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
Environmental groups petitioned EPA to regulate greenhouse gases from new motor vehicles. EPA denied the petition in 2003, saying the Act did not cover climate change and that regulation would be unwise in any event. Massachusetts and other petitioners showed that rising seas were eroding state-owned coastline and that U.S.\ transportation produced over 6\% of global $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

\section{Procedural History}
A fractured D.C.\ Circuit panel denied review, one judge finding no standing. The Supreme Court reversed.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Reversed 5--4. Stevens wrote for Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Roberts dissented on standing and Scalia on the merits, each joined by the other and by Thomas and Alito.

\section{Holding}
A State asserting a statutory procedural right to protect its quasi-sovereign interests receives special solicitude; Massachusetts showed injury, causation, and incremental redressability. Greenhouse gases are ``air pollutants,'' and EPA may not refuse to act on extrastatutory policy grounds.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} States are ``not normal litigants''; agencies may ``whittle away'' at large problems; the Act's repeated ``any'' is unambiguous; and EPA's reasons were ``divorced from the statutory text.'' \textbf{Roberts:} global warming is ``harmful to humanity at large,'' and the ruling is ``SCRAP for a new generation.'' \textbf{Scalia:} EPA deserved deference, and ``air pollutant'' need not cover everything airborne.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a State challenging federal inaction that harms its tidal lands and fisheries. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} a private group alone alleging future climate harm. \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} a State claiming lost revenue from federal deregulation.

\section{Critique}
Critics say ``special solicitude'' lacks constitutional basis and spawned partisan state litigation, and that the Court stretched a local air statute to global climate. Supporters praise its check on executive nullification but concede the doctrine was never defined.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``Given that procedural right and Massachusetts' stake in protecting its quasi-sovereign interests, the Commonwealth is entitled to special solicitude in our standing analysis.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Justice Stevens, for the Court, 549 U.S.\ at 520.
