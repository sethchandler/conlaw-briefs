{\LARGE \textit{Shaw v. Reno}, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
A redistricting plan so bizarre that it is unexplainable except by race states an Equal Protection claim triggering strict scrutiny, even without vote dilution (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/630/}{509 U.S.\ 630}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
After a Justice Department objection under \S\ 5 of the Voting Rights Act, North Carolina drew a second majority-Black congressional district, District 12, which snaked about 160 miles along Interstate 85, often no wider than the highway, splitting 10 counties. Five Durham County voters sued, alleging racial segregation rather than vote dilution.

\section{Procedural History}
A three-judge district court dismissed, reading \textit{UJO v.\ Carey} to require discriminatory purpose and underrepresentation.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Reversed, 5--4. O'Connor wrote for Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas; White (joined by Blackmun and Stevens), Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter dissented.

\section{Holding}
Alleging a plan so irrational on its face that it can be understood only as racial segregation states a claim; if uncontradicted, strict scrutiny applies.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
O'Connor held that racial classifications are ``by their very nature odious to a free people'' and that disregard of compactness and political subdivisions is objective evidence of racial sorting. Such sorting reinforces stereotypes and sends a ``pernicious'' message to representatives; \textit{UJO} involved dilution in a traditional plan. White found no injury since whites remained a majority in 10 of 12 districts; Stevens and Souter saw no harm absent dilution, and Blackmun noted the plan elected North Carolina's first Black representatives since Reconstruction. \textit{Miller v.\ Johnson} later made race's predominance the test.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a district drawn to a 50\% racial target triggers strict scrutiny (\textit{Cooper v.\ Harris}). \textbf{Opposite-Side:} partisan line-drawing defeats a \textit{Shaw} claim (\textit{Alexander}). \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} a remedial district ordered after a VRA \S\ 2 finding.

\section{Critique}
Critics call ``expressive harm'' aesthetic distaste; defenders invoke individual dignity. Sorting voters by race is nearly fatal, while sorting them by party is nonjusticiable.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``Racial gerrymandering, even for remedial purposes, may balkanize us into competing racial factions; it threatens to carry us further from the goal of a political system in which race no longer matters---a goal that the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments embody, and to which the Nation continues to aspire.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Justice O'Connor, 509 U.S.\ at 657.
