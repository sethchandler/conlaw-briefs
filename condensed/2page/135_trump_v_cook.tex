{\LARGE \textit{Trump v.\ Cook}, 609 U.S.\ 528 (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
Fed Governor Lisa Cook stays in office: her ``for cause'' protection is valid, and the President gave her no notice or chance to respond (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/25a312/}{Justia}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
Federal Reserve Governors serve fourteen-year terms ``unless sooner removed for cause by the President.'' After an FHFA referral accused Governor Cook of pre-office mortgage fraud, President Trump demanded her resignation on social media and then removed her by letter, giving her no notice of the evidence or chance to respond.

\section{Procedural History}
The district court preliminarily enjoined the removal; a divided D.C.\ Circuit denied a stay. After argument, the Supreme Court denied the Government's stay application.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Stay denied 5--4. Roberts wrote for Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, and Jackson; Kavanaugh and Jackson concurred; Thomas, Alito (with Gorsuch), and Barrett dissented.

\section{Holding}
The removal was void because \S~242 requires pretermination notice and an opportunity to respond. ``Cause'' is a ``substantial threshold'' that courts may review, and the Fed's removal protection is consistent with Article~II.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} central-bank independence has deep historical roots; ``cause'' requires serious misconduct with a nexus to official duties, not pretext; and Congress legislated against precedent making notice and hearing essential. \textbf{Kavanaugh:} uncertainty about the Fed would roil markets. \textbf{Jackson:} the equities decide the case. \textbf{Thomas:} the Fed's protection is unconstitutional. \textbf{Alito and Barrett:} the Court decided too much on the emergency docket.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a Governor fired overnight for refusing to cut rates. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} an SEC Commissioner, removable at will under \textit{Slaughter}. \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} a renewed mortgage-based removal after full written process.

\section{Critique}
Critics call the Fed carveout incoherent beside \textit{Slaughter} and the notice rule atextual. Others ask why the logic of independence does not protect other regulators.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``At minimum, Cook was entitled to some explanation of the evidence at issue, some avenue for a response, and a deadline by which a response would be due.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Chief Justice Roberts, for the Court, slip op.\ at 24.
