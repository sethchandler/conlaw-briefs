\begin{center}
{\LARGE \textit{Clapper v.\ Amnesty International USA}, 568 U.S.\ 398 (2013)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
Challengers to FISA Section 702 lacked standing: interception was not ``certainly impending,'' and they could not ``manufacture standing'' through precautions (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/568/398/}{Justia}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
Section 702 (2008) allows targeting of non-U.S.\ persons abroad without individualized probable cause. U.S.\ lawyers, journalists, and human rights groups who communicate with foreign clients and sources sued, alleging they had to curtail communications and travel to meet in person.

\section{Procedural History}
The district court found no standing; the Second Circuit reversed, finding an ``objectively reasonable likelihood'' of interception.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Reversed, 5--4. Alito wrote for Roberts, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas; Breyer dissented, joined by Ginsburg, Sotomayor, and Kagan.

\section{Holding}
Future injury must be certainly impending, not reasonably likely, and self-inflicted costs responding to non-imminent harm do not confer standing.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} Respondents' theory depended on a five-link chain of government targeting choices, FISC approval, successful interception, and their own participation, much of it involving independent actors. Precautions taken ``for the price of a plane ticket'' cannot lower the bar, and a subjective chill is insufficient under \textit{Laird}. \textbf{Dissent:} given the content of respondents' communications, government motive, past monitoring, and NSA capacity, interception was highly likely, and ``certainty is not, and never has been, the touchstone of standing.''

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a patient buys credit monitoring after a ransomware attack that took no data. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} a group facing a repeat complaint before an election commission (\textit{Driehaus}). \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} a news organization whose traffic crosses tapped fiber-optic cables.

\section{Critique}
Critics see a Catch-22 that shields secret surveillance from review, noting that the promised criminal notice was being withheld; supporters praise Article III discipline.

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``In other words, respondents cannot manufacture standing merely by inflicting harm on themselves based on their fears of hypothetical future harm that is not certainly impending.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Justice Alito, for the Court, 568 U.S.\ at 416.
