\begin{center}
{\LARGE \textit{Fauntleroy v. Lum}, 210 U.S. 230 (1908)\par}
\vspace{0.5em}
{\large Case Brief --- Two-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\section{Memory Jogger}
Full faith and credit bars collateral attack on a sister-state judgment for errors of law; only want of jurisdiction may be shown (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/210/230/}{210 U.S. 230}).

\section{Detailed Case Facts}
Two Mississippians made a cotton-futures contract that Mississippi made a crime and declared unenforceable ``by any court.'' An arbitrator ruled against Lum, who refused to pay.

\section{Procedural History}
Served while visiting Missouri, Lum lost a suit on the award after the Missouri court excluded his illegality evidence. Fauntleroy, the assignee, sued on the Missouri judgment in Mississippi, whose Supreme Court refused to enforce it.

\section{Judicial Votes}
Reversed 5--4. Holmes wrote for Fuller, Brewer, Peckham, and Moody; White, with Harlan, McKenna, and Day, dissented.

\section{Holding}
The Mississippi statute set a rule of decision, not a limit on jurisdiction. Because Missouri had jurisdiction, its judgment was conclusive in Mississippi even if it misapprehended Mississippi law.

\section{Analysis of Opinions}
\textbf{Majority:} Jurisdiction is power; merits are duty, and a mistaken judgment is erroneous, not void. Judgments get the same ``credit, validity, and effect'' everywhere; Lum's remedy was appeal in Missouri. Mistakes would be ``rare.'' \textbf{White:} the ruling would ``obliterate all state lines,'' letting each State overthrow its sisters' criminal laws; the Clause codified comity.

\section{Examples: Future Applications}
\textbf{Same-Side:} a judgment on a wager illegal in the home State must be enforced there. \textbf{Opposite-Side:} a penal judgment may be refused (\textit{Huntington v. Attrill}). \textbf{Fence-Sitter:} a private bounty judgment.

\section{Critique}
The rule rewards ``tag'' jurisdiction and forum-shopping, and a State's policy evaporates the moment a sister State errs. Modern culture-war federalism tests Holmes's prediction that conflicts would be ``rare.''

\section{Key Quotations}
\begin{quote}
``A judgment is conclusive as to all the \textit{media concludendi}, \ldots{} and it needs no authority to show that it cannot be impeached either in or out of the state by showing that it was based upon a mistake of law. Of course, a want of jurisdiction over either the person or the subject matter might be shown. \ldots{} But, as the jurisdiction of the Missouri court is not open to dispute, the judgment cannot be impeached in Mississippi even if it went upon a misapprehension of the Mississippi law.''
\end{quote}
\noindent --- Justice Holmes, 210 U.S. at 237.
