# Choosing each case's signature quotation (automatic, no human review)

The 1-page and 2-page editions carry one quotation; the 4-page edition carries three. The first must be the
passage the case is *known and cited for*, which is not always the line that best states the holding.

## Method

1. **Candidates.** The full brief's Key Quotations (already verified against the official reports). An agent may
   nominate one famous passage the brief lacks; it is used only if found in the official opinion text and verified.
2. **Citation count.** For each candidate, pick its two or three best-known phrases (the words courts actually
   quote) and count the opinions on CourtListener that quote each phrase exactly. Score = the highest count.
   Never use stock language that predates the case (for example, "a government of laws, and not of men").
3. **Recent cases.** If the top candidate has fewer than 10 quoting opinions (typically 2024–2026 decisions),
   rank by judgment instead: two independent reviewers choose the passage that states the case's rule or is
   most quoted in the scholarship cited in the brief's Critique; a third breaks a disagreement.
4. **Apply.** 1-page and 2-page editions: the top-ranked quotation. 4-page edition: the top three, in rank order.
5. **Log.** Every case's candidates, phrases, counts, and choice are recorded in a table for later reference.
