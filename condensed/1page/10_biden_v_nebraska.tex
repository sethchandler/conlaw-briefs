{\Large \textit{Biden v.\ Nebraska}, 600 U.S.\ 477 (2023)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The student-loan forgiveness case: state standing through MOHELA and the limits of ``waive or modify'' (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-506}{600 U.S. 477}).

\textbf{Facts.} Invoking COVID--19 and the HEROES Act's power to waive or modify Title IV provisions in an emergency, the Secretary of Education announced cancellation of up to \$10,000 (\$20,000 for Pell recipients) for borrowers under \$125,000, costing about \$430 billion. Six States sued; Missouri relied on lost fees to MOHELA, its state-created loan servicer.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court dismissed for lack of standing; the Eighth Circuit enjoined the plan; the Court granted certiorari before judgment.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 6--3. Roberts wrote for the Court; Barrett concurred; Kagan dissented, joined by Sotomayor and Jackson.

\textbf{Holding.} Injury to a state instrumentality the State creates, controls, and may dissolve is injury to the State. ``Waive or modify'' permits only modest adjustments, and a program of vast economic significance needs clear congressional authorization.

\textbf{Reasoning.} MOHELA's \$44 million loss was Missouri's own under \textit{Arkansas v. Texas} and \textit{Lebron}. Following \textit{MCI}, ``modify'' means moderate change; the Secretary built a new scheme rather than waiving any requirement, and \textit{West Virginia v. EPA} demanded clear authorization for so costly and salient a plan. Barrett recast the Major Questions Doctrine as a contextual reading tool (the babysitter analogy). Kagan called the States ideological plaintiffs and read ``waive or modify'' as authorizing a spectrum of changes.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a \$350 billion farm-debt cancellation under a similar clause fails. \emph{Opposite-Side:} targeted hurricane deadline waivers are valid. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a three-year payment and interest pause.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics say the MOHELA theory erodes Article III limits and the majority rewrote broad text; empirical scholars and conservative textualists question Barrett's contextual defense.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``We hold today that the Act allows the Secretary to `waive or modify' existing statutory or regulatory provisions applicable to financial assistance programs under the Education Act, not to rewrite that statute from the ground up.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, Opinion of the Court, 600 U.S. 477.
