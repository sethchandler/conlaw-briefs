\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{District of Columbia v. Heller}, 554 U.S. 570 (2008)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The individual right to keep and bear arms; D.C.'s handgun ban falls (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/554/570/}{554 U.S.\ 570}).

\textbf{Facts.} D.C.\ effectively banned civilian handguns and required other home firearms to be kept ``unloaded and disassembled or bound by a trigger lock or similar device.'' Dick Heller, a special police officer, was denied registration of a handgun for home defense.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court dismissed; the D.C.\ Circuit reversed, 2--1.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 5--4 (Scalia for the Court; Stevens and Breyer dissenting, joined by Souter and Ginsburg).

\textbf{Holding.} The Second Amendment guarantees a pre-existing individual right, unconnected with militia service, whose core is self-defense in the home. Banning handguns, an entire class of arms in common use for self-defense, and requiring home firearms to be inoperable fail under any standard of scrutiny.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The prefatory militia clause announces a purpose but cannot limit the operative clause; ``the right of the people'' is individual, and ``bear arms'' meant carrying weapons for confrontation. \textit{Miller} protects arms ``in common use at the time.'' The right is ``not unlimited,'' but interest balancing is rejected. Stevens read the Amendment as protecting state militias only; Breyer would uphold the ban under an ``interest-balancing inquiry.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} Chicago's handgun ban (\textit{McDonald}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} disarming domestic abusers under restraining orders (\textit{Rahimi}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} AR-15 and large-magazine bans.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics call the history selective and the ``presumptively lawful'' exceptions unsupported; originalists praise the method but fault the missing standard of review.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``[N]othing in our opinion should be taken to cast doubt on longstanding prohibitions on the possession of firearms by felons and the mentally ill, or laws forbidding the carrying of firearms in sensitive places such as schools and government buildings, or laws imposing conditions and qualifications on the commercial sale of arms.'' --- Justice Scalia, 554 U.S.\ at 626--627.
