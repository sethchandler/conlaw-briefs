\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{New York v. United States}, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The radioactive-waste ``take title'' case: Congress may not commandeer state legislatures (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112768/new-york-v-united-states/}{505 U.S. 144}).

\textbf{Facts.} A 1985 compromise statute pressed States to provide for their low-level radioactive waste through monetary incentives, access penalties, and a duty to take title to waste and assume liability if they failed. New York sought to site a facility amid local opposition.

\textbf{Procedural History.} New York sued under the Tenth Amendment; the lower courts dismissed.

\textbf{Votes.} 9--0 upholding the incentives; 6--3 striking take title. O'Connor wrote for the Court; White and Stevens dissented in part.

\textbf{Holding.} Congress may encourage States through conditional spending or conditional preemption, but it may not compel them to enact and enforce a federal program; the take-title provision is invalid and severable.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The Framers chose direct regulation of individuals over governing States, and commandeering blurs political accountability. Take title offered only two coercive choices, and New York's earlier consent could not waive a structural protection of liberty. White saw a state-negotiated bargain; Stevens rejected the rule entirely.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} barring legislatures from repealing gambling bans. \emph{Opposite-Side:} EPA regulating private factories when a State declines. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} duties on state child-welfare agencies.

\textbf{Critique.} The rule, O'Connor conceded, ``is not derived from the text of the Tenth Amendment itself.''

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In providing for a stronger central government, therefore, the Framers explicitly chose a Constitution that confers upon Congress the power to regulate individuals, not States.'' --- Justice O'Connor, Opinion of the Court.
