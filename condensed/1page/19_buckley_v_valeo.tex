\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Buckley v.\ Valeo}, 424 U.S.\ 1 (1976)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Contribution limits and disclosure upheld; expenditure limits struck; FEC appointments by Congress invalid (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/424/1/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} The post-Watergate FECA amendments capped contributions and all forms of campaign spending, required donor disclosure, publicly financed presidential races, and created an FEC mostly appointed by congressional leaders. Senator Buckley and a diverse coalition sued.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The en banc D.C.\ Circuit upheld nearly all of FECA; the Supreme Court affirmed in part and reversed in part.

\textbf{Votes.} Per curiam with shifting majorities: contribution limits upheld 6--2; expenditure limits struck 7--1; FEC appointments struck 7--1.

\textbf{Holding.} Contribution limits are valid if closely drawn to preventing \textit{quid pro quo} corruption or its appearance; expenditure limits fail exacting scrutiny; disclosure and voluntary public financing are valid; officers with significant federal authority must be appointed under Article~II.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Spending money is speech, but a contribution is a ``general expression of support'' with corruption risk, while independent expenditures pose a ``substantially diminished potential for abuse.'' Burger: ``contributions and expenditures are two sides of the same First Amendment coin.'' White: ``money is not speech.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a state \$1,075 contribution cap. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a harassed minor party exempt from disclosure. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} matching funds triggered by an opponent's spending.

\textbf{Critique.} The contribution--expenditure line is attacked from both left and right as unstable.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``[T]he concept that government may restrict the speech of some elements of our society in order to enhance the relative voice of others is wholly foreign to the First Amendment.'' --- Per Curiam, 424 U.S.\ at 48--49.
