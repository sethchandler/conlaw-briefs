\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{McDonald v. City of Chicago}, 561 U.S. 742 (2010)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The Chicago handgun case: the Second Amendment applies to the States (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/149702/mcdonald-v-city-of-chicago/}{561 U.S. 742}).

\textbf{Facts.} After \textit{Heller}, Chicago residents challenged city handgun bans that kept them from keeping handguns at home for self-defense.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The Seventh Circuit, bound by nineteenth-century precedent, upheld the bans; the Supreme Court granted certiorari.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--4. Alito wrote for the Court and a four-Justice plurality; Thomas concurred in the judgment; Stevens and Breyer dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The Fourteenth Amendment incorporates the Second Amendment right of armed self-defense, which is fundamental to ordered liberty and deeply rooted in history, and it applies fully to the States.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Self-defense is ``the central component'' of the right, and Reconstruction Congresses sought to protect freedmen's arms. The plurality used the Due Process Clause and declined to revive Privileges or Immunities; Thomas called substantive due process a ``legal fiction.'' Stevens and Breyer stressed local experimentation and judicial incompetence on gun policy.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a public-housing handgun ban. \emph{Opposite-Side:} felon-possession bans. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a ban for lawful permanent residents.

\textbf{Critique.} Originalists fault the refusal to overrule \textit{Slaughter-House}; critics note the ``voting paradox'' of the 4--1--4 split.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In \textit{Heller}, we held that the Second Amendment protects the right to possess a handgun in the home for the purpose of self-defense. Unless considerations of \textit{stare decisis} counsel otherwise, a provision of the Bill of Rights that protects a right that is fundamental from an American perspective applies equally to the Federal Government and the States. See \textit{Duncan}, 391 U.S., at 149, and n.~14. We therefore hold that the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment incorporates the Second Amendment right recognized in \textit{Heller}.'' --- Justice Alito, Plurality Opinion, 561 U.S. at 791.
