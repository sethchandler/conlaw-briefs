\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Regents of the University of California v. Bakke}, 438 U.S. 265 (1978)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Racial quotas are out, but race may be a ``plus'' factor for diversity (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/438/265/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} UC Davis Medical School reserved 16 of 100 seats for a separately reviewed special program for disadvantaged and minority applicants. Allan Bakke, a white applicant with strong scores, was twice rejected while special admittees with lower averages were admitted.

\textbf{Procedural History.} California courts held the program unlawful, barred any use of race, and ordered Bakke admitted. The Supreme Court affirmed in part and reversed in part.

\textbf{Votes.} 4--1--4: Powell announced the judgment, joining the Stevens bloc (Burger, Stewart, Rehnquist) on the quota and the Brennan bloc (White, Marshall, Blackmun) on considering race.

\textbf{Holding.} Title VI tracks Equal Protection; the quota is unlawful and Bakke must be admitted; race may be one factor in admissions.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Powell applied strict scrutiny to all racial classifications, rejected remedying ``societal discrimination'' but accepted diversity, and endorsed Harvard's ``plus'' approach. The Brennan bloc would apply intermediate scrutiny and uphold the program; the Stevens bloc found Title VI ``crystal clear.'' Blackmun: ``In order to get beyond racism, we must first take account of race.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} an automatic minority point bonus. \emph{Opposite-Side:} holistic ``plus'' review. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} race added to a Top 10\% plan.

\textbf{Critique.} Scalia called preferences \textit{The Disease as Cure}; antisubordination scholars fault Powell's symmetry.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The guarantee of equal protection cannot mean one thing when applied to one individual and something else when applied to a person of another color. If both are not accorded the same protection, then it is not equal.'' --- Justice Powell, 438 U.S. at 289--290.
