\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Gonzales v. Raich}, 545 U.S. 1 (2005)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Congress may ban homegrown, state-legal medical marijuana as part of a comprehensive interstate drug regime (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/799995/gonzales-v-raich/}{CourtListener}).

\textbf{Facts.} California's Compassionate Use Act shielded seriously ill patients using marijuana on a physician's recommendation. Angel Raich and Diane Monson relied on cannabis as their only effective treatment. DEA agents seized and destroyed Monson's six plants under the Controlled Substances Act (CSA), which lists marijuana in Schedule I.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court denied a preliminary injunction; the Ninth Circuit reversed under \textit{Lopez} and \textit{Morrison}. The Supreme Court vacated and remanded.

\textbf{Votes.} 6--3: Stevens for the Court; Scalia concurred in the judgment; O'Connor (with Rehnquist and Thomas in part) and Thomas dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The CSA validly reaches intrastate, noncommercial medical marijuana: Congress may regulate local activity when it rationally concludes that exempting it would undercut a comprehensive scheme regulating an interstate market.

\textbf{Reasoning.} As in \textit{Wickard v. Filburn}, home production of a fungible commodity affects the national market; exempting it would leave a ``gaping hole'' in the CSA. \textit{Lopez} and \textit{Morrison} involved single-subject statutes regulating noneconomic conduct. Scalia relied on the Necessary and Proper Clause. O'Connor warned that \textit{Lopez} becomes a drafting guide; Thomas, that Congress may now ``regulate quilting bees, clothes drives, and potluck suppers throughout the 50 States.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} home-assembled machine guns. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a standalone ban on backyard composting. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a one-county cave beetle under the Endangered Species Act.

\textbf{Critique.} Originalists say \textit{Raich} abandoned the founding meaning of ``commerce''; \textit{NFIB v. Sebelius} later cabined it.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``We need not determine whether respondents' activities, taken in the aggregate, substantially affect interstate commerce in fact, but only whether a `rational basis' exists for so concluding.'' --- Justice Stevens, 545 U.S. 1.
