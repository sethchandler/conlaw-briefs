\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs}, 538 U.S. 721 (2003)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The FMLA's family-care provision validly abrogates state sovereign immunity under Section 5 (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/538/721/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Nevada fired William Hibbs after he used 12 weeks of FMLA leave to care for his injured wife and did not return; he sued the State for damages.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court held the suit barred by the Eleventh Amendment; the Ninth Circuit reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 6--3 (Rehnquist for the Court; Souter and Stevens concurring; Scalia and Kennedy dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} The family-care provision is congruent and proportional Section 5 legislation. Because sex classifications get heightened scrutiny, Congress can more easily show a pattern of unconstitutional state conduct than for age or disability.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The record showed state leave policies built on stereotypes that women are caregivers and men are not; 15 States gave women extended maternity leave and only four gave men parallel leave. A gender-neutral floor with unpaid leave, eligibility limits, and capped damages fit the problem. \emph{Separately:} Stevens relied on the commerce power; Scalia said ``[t]here is no guilt by association'' among States; Kennedy called the FMLA a benefit program on a thin record.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} ADA courthouse-access suits. \emph{Opposite-Side:} FMLA self-care suits (\textit{Coleman}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} pregnancy-accommodation damages suits.

\textbf{Critique.} Faulted for relaxing \textit{Boerne}; praised for ``feminist universalism.''

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Stereotypes about women's domestic roles are reinforced by parallel stereotypes presuming a lack of domestic responsibilities for men. Because employers continued to regard the family as the woman's domain, they often denied men similar accommodations or discouraged them from taking leave. These mutually reinforcing stereotypes created a self-fulfilling cycle of discrimination that forced women to continue to assume the role of primary family caregiver.'' --- Chief Justice Rehnquist, 538 U.S. at 736.
