\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner}, 387 U.S. 136 (1967)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Final agency action is presumptively reviewable; pre-enforcement challenges are ripe when fit and delay causes hardship (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/387/136/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} An FDA ``every time'' rule required drug labels to print the generic name with each appearance of the trade name, on pain of seizure and prosecution. Thirty-seven manufacturers sued before any enforcement.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court held the rule unauthorized; the Third Circuit reversed, finding review precluded.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed and remanded, 5--3 (Harlan for the Court; Fortas, Warren, and Clark dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} Review of final agency action is presumed absent ``clear and convincing evidence'' of contrary congressional intent; the challenge was ripe because the issue was purely legal, the rule final, and delay a real hardship.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The APA embodies a ``basic presumption of judicial review,'' and the Act's special review provision did not displace other remedies. The formal, immediately effective rule forced makers to choose between costly relabeling and risking prosecution, seizures, and reputational harm in a ``sensitive industry.'' An industry-wide suit would speed, not stall, uniform enforcement. \emph{Separately:} Fortas warned of a ``roving commission to halt the regulatory process.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a final rule requiring daily data deletion. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a discretionary audit rule (\textit{Toilet Goods}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} guidance backed by warning letters.

\textbf{Critique.} Blurs Article III and prudential ripeness; shifts power from agencies to judges.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The problem is best seen in a twofold aspect, requiring us to evaluate both the fitness of the issues for judicial decision and the hardship to the parties of withholding court consideration.'' --- Justice Harlan, 387 U.S. at 149.
