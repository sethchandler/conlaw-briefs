\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Fulton v. City of Philadelphia}, 593 U.S. 522 (2021)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} A discretionary-exemption clause sank Philadelphia's demand that a Catholic agency certify same-sex foster couples (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4892570/fulton-v-philadelphia/}{CourtListener}).

\textbf{Facts.} Catholic Social Services would not certify same-sex married couples as foster parents; none had applied. The City froze referrals and refused to renew its contract, citing a nondiscrimination clause allowing exceptions in the Commissioner's ``sole discretion.''

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court and Third Circuit upheld the policy under \textit{Smith}.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 9--0: Roberts for six; Barrett concurring; Alito and Gorsuch (with Thomas) concurring in the judgment.

\textbf{Holding.} A formal mechanism for individualized exemptions, even unused, makes a policy not generally applicable and triggers strict scrutiny, which the City failed. \textit{Smith} was not overruled.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Foster certification is not a public accommodation, and the City had no compelling interest in denying CSS an exception. Barrett doubted \textit{Smith} but feared its replacement; Alito would overrule it, warning the City could delete the clause; Gorsuch accused the majority of ``dodging.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a never-used ``good cause'' waiver. \emph{Opposite-Side:} an exception-free rule. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} an exception-free rule adopted amid officials' hostility.

\textbf{Critique.} Treating a boilerplate clause as fatal hollows out \textit{Smith} without the candor of overruling it.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The creation of a formal mechanism for granting exceptions renders a policy not generally applicable, regardless whether any exceptions have been given, because it `invite[s]' the government to decide which reasons for not complying with the policy are worthy of solicitude, \textit{Smith}, 494 U.S. at 884---here, at the Commissioner's `sole discretion.'\,'' --- Chief Justice Roberts.
