\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States}, 295 U.S. 495 (1935)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The ``sick chicken case'': NIRA's codes were an unconstitutional delegation and reached beyond the commerce power (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/295/495/}{295 U.S. 495}).

\textbf{Facts.} NIRA let the President approve criminally enforced ``codes of fair competition'' proposed by trade groups. The Schechters, Brooklyn kosher poultry slaughterers who sold only locally, were prosecuted under the Live Poultry Code.

\textbf{Procedural History.} Convicted on 19 counts, they won reversal in the Second Circuit only on the wage-and-hour counts.

\textbf{Votes.} 9--0. Hughes wrote for the Court; Cardozo, joined by Stone, concurred.

\textbf{Holding.} Section 3 unconstitutionally delegates legislative power without standards, and applying the code to local slaughtering and sales exceeds the commerce power.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Emergencies do not enlarge constitutional power. Congress must set policy and standards, but NIRA left ``fair competition'' undefined and let private groups and the President make law. The poultry had come to rest within the State, so its handling affected commerce only indirectly. Cardozo called \S~3 a ``roving commission.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} industry-drafted AI codes. \emph{Opposite-Side:} health-based air standards (\textit{Whitman}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} criminally enforced ``fair and orderly markets'' rules.

\textbf{Critique.} The decision targets corporatist self-regulation, but ``how much is too much'' is hard for courts to judge.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The delegated power of legislation which has found expression in this code is not canalized within banks that keep it from overflowing. It is unconfined and vagrant \dots\ This is delegation running riot. No such plenitude of power is susceptible of transfer.'' --- Justice Cardozo, concurring, 295 U.S. at 551, 553.
