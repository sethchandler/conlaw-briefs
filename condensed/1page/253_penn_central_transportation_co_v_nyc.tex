\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Penn Central Transportation Co.\ v.\ New York City}, 438 U.S.\ 104 (1978)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} No taking in barring a tower above Grand Central Terminal; the default regulatory-takings test (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/438/104/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} New York designated Grand Central Terminal a landmark and rejected Penn Central's plans for a 53- or 55-story office tower above it. Landmark owners could transfer unused air rights (TDRs).

\textbf{Procedural History.} The New York Court of Appeals found no taking.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 6--3: Brennan for the Court; Rehnquist, with Burger and Stevens, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} No taking: courts weigh economic impact, investment-backed expectations, and the character of the action, measured against the parcel as a whole.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Air rights cannot be severed; the citywide program was not spot zoning; and the Terminal kept its existing use, a reasonable return, and valuable TDRs. Rehnquist saw a few owners singled out because ``too good a job was done'' in building.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a tower denied on a profitable landmark. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a public dock easement (\textit{Cedar Point}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} landmarking one small building without TDRs.

\textbf{Critique.} The unweighted factors are unpredictable and tilt toward regulators, and the ``parcel as a whole'' denominator, often decisive, remains fluid after \textit{Murr}.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In engaging in these essentially ad hoc, factual inquiries, the Court's decisions have identified several factors that have particular significance. The economic impact of the regulation on the claimant and, particularly, the extent to which the regulation has interfered with distinct investment-backed expectations are, of course, relevant considerations.'' --- Justice Brennan, 438 U.S.\ at 124.
