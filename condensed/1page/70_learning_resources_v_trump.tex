{\Large \textit{Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump}, 607 U.S. \_\_\_ (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The IEEPA tariff case: emergency power to ``regulate \ldots{} importation'' is not a power to tax (\href{https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf}{Slip Op.}).

\textbf{Facts.} In 2025 President Trump invoked IEEPA to impose ``drug trafficking'' tariffs on Canada, Mexico, and China and worldwide ``reciprocal'' tariffs, collecting more than \$200 billion. Importers, including two family-owned education-products companies, and twelve States sued.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The D.C. district court, the Court of International Trade, and the en banc Federal Circuit ruled against the tariffs; the Supreme Court consolidated the cases.

\textbf{Votes.} 6--3; Roberts wrote for the Court, but only Gorsuch and Barrett joined his major-questions analysis; Kavanaugh, Thomas, and Alito dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. The tariff power belongs to Congress, and ``regulate'' does not encompass revenue-raising duties.

\textbf{Reasoning.} IEEPA never mentions tariffs, ``regulate'' does not ordinarily mean tax, and its neighboring verbs describe sanctions. The plurality added the Major Questions Doctrine, which Gorsuch called ``pro-Congress'' and Barrett called ordinary textualism; Kagan found ordinary construction enough. Kavanaugh replied that tariffs are a classic means of regulating importation.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} an IEEPA carbon border tax falls. \emph{Opposite-Side:} IEEPA asset freezes and embargoes stand. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a cost-recovery licensing fee on imported AI models.

\textbf{Critique.} Commentators see a ``canon war'' over the Major Questions Doctrine, which the fractured votes left ``more tenuous.''

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Based on two words separated by 16 others in Section 1702(a)(1)(B) of IEEPA---`regulate' and `importation'---the President asserts the independent power to impose tariffs on imports from any country, of any product, at any rate, for any amount of time. Those words cannot bear such weight.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, Opinion of the Court, slip op. at 5.
