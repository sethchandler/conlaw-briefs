\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{California v. Texas}, 593 U.S. 659 (2021)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The \$0 individual mandate: no one has standing to challenge an unenforceable provision (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4892571/california-v-texas/}{593 U.S.\ 659}).

\textbf{Facts.} \textit{NFIB v.\ Sebelius} upheld the ACA's individual mandate as a tax. In 2017 Congress cut the penalty to \$0. Texas, 17 States, and two individuals sued, arguing the mandate was now unconstitutional and inseverable from the whole ACA; California and other States intervened to defend it.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court struck the mandate and the entire ACA; the Fifth Circuit affirmed in part and remanded on severability.

\textbf{Votes.} Dismissed, 7--2 (Breyer for seven; Thomas concurring; Alito, joined by Gorsuch, dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} No plaintiff showed an injury fairly traceable to actual or threatened enforcement of \S\ 5000A(a). Unenforceable statutory text cannot by itself support traceability or redressability, so the Court did not reach the merits or severability.

\textbf{Reasoning.} With a \$0 penalty, no officer could enforce the mandate against the individuals, and remedies run against officials, not abstract rules. The States' enrollment theory rested on unproven third-party choices, and their reporting costs came from separate, unchallenged provisions. Standing-through-inseverability was forfeited, though Thomas suggested it might work later. Alito, dissenting, found concrete state costs and would have held the mandate unconstitutional and inseverable.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a \$0-penalty composting directive. \emph{Opposite-Side:} an IRS assessment collected by refund offset. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a timely pleaded inseverability theory.

\textbf{Critique.} The Court ignored \textit{Massachusetts v.\ EPA}'s ``special solicitude'' for States; some saw principled restraint, others selective rules shielding the ACA.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``To find standing here to attack an unenforceable statutory provision would allow a federal court to issue what would amount to `an advisory opinion without the possibility of any judicial relief.' '' --- Justice Breyer, for the Court.
