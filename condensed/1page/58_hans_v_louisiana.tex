\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Hans v. Louisiana}, 134 U.S. 1 (1890)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Sovereign immunity beyond the Eleventh Amendment's text: no federal suit by a citizen against his own State (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/134/1/}{134 U.S. 1}).

\textbf{Facts.} Louisiana's 1879 constitution repudiated coupons on its 1874 ``consolidated bonds.'' Bernard Hans, a Louisiana citizen, sued the State in federal court for \$87,500, claiming a Contract Clause violation.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The federal circuit court dismissed for want of the State's consent; Hans brought a writ of error.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 9--0 (8--1 on reasoning): Bradley for the Court; Harlan concurred in the judgment only.

\textbf{Holding.} An unconsenting State cannot be sued in federal court by its own citizen, even under federal law, because the Constitution presupposes immunity not surrendered in the plan of the Convention.

\textbf{Reasoning.} A literal reading would produce an ``anomalous result,'' letting citizens bring claims outsiders could not. \textit{Chisholm} caused ``such a shock of surprise throughout the country'' that the Amendment followed, and Hamilton and Madison had assured ratifiers of immunity. The 1875 Act's jurisdiction was only ``concurrent with the courts of the several States,'' which could not hear such suits. Harlan rejected the attack on \textit{Chisholm} as unnecessary.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a damages suit against a State under a Commerce Clause statute is barred (\textit{Seminole Tribe}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} a prospective injunction against a state officer (\textit{Ex parte Young}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} suits authorized under war or eminent-domain powers.

\textbf{Critique.} Diversity theorists such as William Fletcher and Akhil Amar say \textit{Hans} defies the text, which repealed only diversity jurisdiction; Baude, Sachs, and Nelson defend it as background common law; progressives see a post-Reconstruction retreat from Redeemer bond repudiation that leaves rights without remedies.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The suability of a State without its consent was a thing unknown to the law.'' --- Justice Bradley, Opinion of the Court, 134 U.S. at 16.
