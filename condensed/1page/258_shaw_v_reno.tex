\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Shaw v. Reno}, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} North Carolina's I-85 district: racial gerrymanders face strict scrutiny (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/630/}{509 U.S.\ 630}).

\textbf{Facts.} After a Justice Department objection under the Voting Rights Act, North Carolina drew a second majority-Black district that snaked about 160 miles along Interstate 85, splitting 10 counties. Five Durham County voters sued, alleging racial segregation, not vote dilution.

\textbf{Procedural History.} A three-judge district court dismissed under \textit{UJO v.\ Carey}.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--4 (O'Connor for the Court; White, Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} A plan so irrational on its face that it can be understood only as an effort to segregate voters by race states an Equal Protection claim and, if uncontradicted, triggers strict scrutiny.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Racial classifications are ``by their very nature odious to a free people,'' and disregard of compactness and political lines is objective evidence of racial sorting, which reinforces stereotypes and sends a ``pernicious'' message to representatives. The dissenters saw no injury without vote dilution. \textit{Miller v.\ Johnson} later made racial predominance the test.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a district built to a racial target. \emph{Opposite-Side:} partisan line-drawing (\textit{Alexander}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a court-ordered remedial district.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics call the harm aesthetic; race-based sorting is nearly fatal while party-based sorting is nonjusticiable.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Racial gerrymandering, even for remedial purposes, may balkanize us into competing racial factions; it threatens to carry us further from the goal of a political system in which race no longer matters---a goal that the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments embody, and to which the Nation continues to aspire.'' --- Justice O'Connor, 509 U.S.\ at 657.
