\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{United States v.\ Brown}, 381 U.S.\ 437 (1965)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Barring Communist Party members from union office was a bill of attainder (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/381/437/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Section 504 of the 1959 Labor-Management Reporting and Disclosure Act made it a crime for current or recent Communist Party members to serve as union officers. Archie Brown, an open Party member for over twenty-five years, served on a San Francisco longshoremen's board.

\textbf{Procedural History.} Convicted and sentenced to six months; the en banc Ninth Circuit reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed 5--4: Warren for the Court; White, with Clark, Harlan, and Stewart, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Congress may not single out an ascertainable group for punishment without trial, even for preventive ends; \S~504 is a bill of attainder.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The Clause implements the separation of powers. Congress may weed dangerous persons out of unions but must use general rules that courts apply, not designate a party; membership is no ``shorthand'' for dangerousness, and preventive aims are still punishment. White saw a per se rule threatening many regulatory laws.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a felony bar on a named protest group's members. \emph{Opposite-Side:} disqualification after an individualized hearing. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} defunding a named nonprofit.

\textbf{Critique.} After \textit{Nixon v.\ Administrator of General Services}, \textit{Brown} may survive mainly as a bar on criminalizing party status.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The best available evidence, the writings of the architects of our constitutional system, indicates that the Bill of Attainder Clause was intended not as a narrow, technical (and therefore soon to be outmoded) prohibition, but rather as an implementation of the separation of powers, a general safeguard against legislative exercise of the judicial function, or, more simply, trial by legislature.'' --- Chief Justice Warren, 381 U.S.\ at 442.
