\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Baker v.\ Carr}, 369 U.S.\ 186 (1962)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Malapportionment challenges to state legislatures are justiciable; the six-factor political question test (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/369/186/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Tennessee had not reapportioned its legislature since 1901 despite a constitutional duty to do so every decade. As the State urbanized, about 37\% of voters elected 20 of 33 Senators. Urban voters sued state officials under \S~1983, alleging a ``debasement of their votes'' in violation of Equal Protection; Tennessee offered no initiative, referendum, or state judicial remedy.

\textbf{Procedural History.} A three-judge district court dismissed under \textit{Colegrove v. Green}. The Supreme Court reversed and remanded.

\textbf{Votes.} 6--2: Brennan for the Court, with concurrences by Douglas, Clark, and Stewart; Frankfurter (with Harlan) and Harlan dissented; Whittaker did not participate.

\textbf{Holding.} Federal courts have jurisdiction, voters alleging vote dilution have standing, and an Equal Protection malapportionment claim is not a political question. The merits were left for remand.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The political question doctrine concerns the judiciary's relationship to coordinate federal branches, not to the States. Guaranty Clause claims fail only because they implicate the six factors; this claim invoked ``well developed and familiar'' standards. Clark called the map ``a crazy quilt without rational basis.'' Frankfurter saw a ``Guarantee Clause claim masquerading under a different label''; Harlan found no duty of equal weighting.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} Alabama's 41-to-1 county scheme (\textit{Reynolds v. Sims}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} Senate impeachment-trial procedure (\textit{Nixon v. United States}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a War Powers suit over prolonged airstrikes.

\textbf{Critique.} The promised manageable standards were ipse dixit until \textit{Reynolds}, and the same prong later shielded partisan gerrymandering in \textit{Rucho}.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Prominent on the surface of any case held to involve a political question is found a textually demonstrable constitutional commitment of the issue to a coordinate political department; or a lack of judicially discoverable and manageable standards for resolving it \ldots'' --- Justice Brennan, 369 U.S. at 217.
