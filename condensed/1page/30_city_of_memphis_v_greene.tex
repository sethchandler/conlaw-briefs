\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{City of Memphis v.\ Greene}, 451 U.S.\ 100 (1981)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Closing a street between white and Black neighborhoods to cut through traffic violated neither 42 U.S.C.\ \S~1982 nor the Thirteenth Amendment (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/451/100/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} At the request of residents of all-white Hein Park, Memphis closed the north end of West Drive, which carried traffic from a predominantly Black area, citing through traffic and children's safety. Black residents protested that it shut the door on them.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court found disparate impact but no intent; the Sixth Circuit reversed, calling the closure a ``badge of slavery.''

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 6--3: Stevens for the Court; White concurred in the judgment; Marshall, with Brennan and Blackmun, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} A neutral closure, without intent, property depreciation, blocked access, or unequal benefits, impairs no \S~1982 right and is no badge of slavery.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Drivers merely turned a few blocks earlier, property values held, and no comparable benefit was denied to Black neighborhoods; ordinary traffic rules inevitably have disparate effects. White would have held that \S~1982 requires intent. Marshall saw a ``monument to racial hostility'' at the racial dividing line.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} speed humps to curb cut-through traffic. \emph{Opposite-Side:} cul-de-sacs granted only to white subdivisions. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a solid wall facing a Black apartment complex.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics say the Court ducked the intent question and froze Thirteenth Amendment doctrine.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``We merely hold that the impact of the closing of West Drive on nonresidents of Hein Park is a routine burden of citizenship; it does not reflect a violation of the Thirteenth Amendment.'' --- Justice Stevens, 451 U.S.\ at 129.
