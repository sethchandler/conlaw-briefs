\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{United States Steel Corp. v. Multistate Tax Commission}, 434 U.S. 452 (1978)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} An interstate tax compact needed no congressional consent because it did not enhance state power against the Federal Government (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/434/452/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} When federal uniformity bills stalled, state tax administrators drafted the Multistate Tax Compact (1966). Its Commission issued advisory rules and audited corporations at a member state's request; each state kept control of rates and assessments and could withdraw at will. Congress never passed any of twelve consent bills.

\textbf{Procedural History.} Multistate taxpayers led by United States Steel sued; a three-judge court granted summary judgment for the Commission.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 7--2: Powell for the Court; White, with Blackmun, dissenting.

\textbf{Holding.} Under \textit{Virginia v.\ Tennessee}, consent is required only for agreements tending to increase state power so as to encroach on federal supremacy; this Compact did not.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The Compact delegated no sovereign power, authorized nothing states could not do alone, allowed withdrawal, and left Congress free to preempt. White said the Court demanded proof of actual harm and that the Clause must bar some joint actions individual states could take.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} an advisory interstate AI commission. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a maritime compact taxing foreign vessels. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} the National Popular Vote Compact.

\textbf{Critique.} The test leaves ``any Agreement or Compact'' with little force, neglects harms to nonmember states, and ignores the synergy of a permanent multistate agency.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``But the test is whether the Compact enhances state power \textit{quoad} the National Government. This pact does not purport to authorize the member States to exercise any powers they could not exercise in its absence.'' --- Justice Powell, 434 U.S. at 473.
