\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Buck v. Bell}, 274 U.S. 200 (1927)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Virginia's compulsory eugenic sterilization law upheld by analogy to compulsory vaccination (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/274/200/}{274 U.S. 200}).

\textbf{Facts.} Virginia's 1924 statute allowed sterilizing institutionalized ``mental defectives'' after notice, a hearing, and appeals. Carrie Buck, eighteen, was recorded as feebleminded, as were her mother and child; later research showed none was intellectually disabled. Her counsel, a former colony board member, called no witnesses.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The colony board, county circuit court, and Virginia Supreme Court of Appeals approved her sterilization.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 8--1: Holmes for the Court; Butler dissented without opinion.

\textbf{Holding.} Sterilizing institutional inmates adjudicated, after careful procedures, to carry hereditary defects is within the police power; confining the law to inmates does not deny equal protection.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The statute's procedures satisfied due process. Deferring to eugenic premises, Holmes reasoned that a state that can conscript its ``best citizens'' may demand ``these lesser sacrifices,'' and dismissed equal protection as ``the usual last resort of constitutional arguments.'' Butler left no reasoning.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a Virginia-style law with hearings and appeals. \emph{Opposite-Side:} summary sterilization without notice or hearing. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} mandated reversible contraception for community-dwelling welfare recipients.

\textbf{Critique.} An anti-canon case resting on a false analogy and a sham record, it fueled over 60,000 sterilizations and has never been formally overruled.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The principle that sustains compulsory vaccination is broad enough to cover cutting the Fallopian tubes. Jacobson v. Massachusetts, 197 U. S. 11. Three generations of imbeciles are enough.'' --- Justice Holmes, 274 U.S. at 207.
