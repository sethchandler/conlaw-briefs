\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{West Virginia v. B. P. J. / Little v. Hecox}, Nos. 24-43 \& 24-38 (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} States may limit girls' and women's school teams to biological females (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-43}{Cornell LII}).

\textbf{Facts.} West Virginia and Idaho barred biological males from female school teams. B. P. J., a transgender girl who never underwent male puberty, and Lindsay Hecox, a transgender college student, challenged the laws.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The Fourth Circuit ruled for B. P. J. on Title IX; the Ninth Circuit affirmed an injunction for Hecox. The Supreme Court reversed both.

\textbf{Votes.} 9--0 in judgment on Title IX, 6--3 on Equal Protection: Kavanaugh for the Court; Thomas and Gorsuch concurred; Sotomayor (with Kagan and Jackson) and Jackson concurred in part and dissented in part.

\textbf{Holding.} ``Sex'' in Title IX means biological sex. The laws classify by sex, satisfy intermediate scrutiny by serving safety and fairness, and need not include individualized exemptions.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Physical differences are ``enduring'' and sports are ``generally zero sum''; an as-applied challenge is a ``backdoor argument for strict scrutiny,'' and courts should defer amid ``ongoing medical and scientific debate.'' Gorsuch stressed clear notice; Sotomayor would have allowed factfinding on a subclass who never underwent male puberty.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} girls' swimming eligibility rules. \emph{Opposite-Side:} expelling a transgender student. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} inclusive state laws (reserved).

\textbf{Critique.} Chemerinsky says the Court diluted intermediate scrutiny; textualists praise its fidelity to 1972 meaning.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Consistent with Title IX and the Equal Protection Clause, we hold that the States may maintain women's and girls' sports for biological females. They may determine eligibility for women's and girls' sports based on biological sex. The Constitution and Title IX do not require an overhaul of women's and girls' sports throughout America.'' --- Justice Kavanaugh, slip op. at 29.
