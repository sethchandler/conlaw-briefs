\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{West Coast Hotel Co. v. Parrish}, 300 U.S. 379 (1937)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The ``switch in time'': the end of \textit{Lochner}-era liberty of contract (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/300/379/}{300 U.S.\ 379}).

\textbf{Facts.} Washington's 1913 law set minimum wages for women, here \$14.50 a week for hotel chambermaids. Elsie Parrish sued the West Coast Hotel Company for \$216.19 in back wages; the hotel invoked freedom of contract under \textit{Adkins}.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The trial court struck the law; the Washington Supreme Court reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 5--4 (Hughes for the Court, with Roberts; Sutherland and the ``Four Horsemen'' dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} The Constitution guarantees no absolute freedom of contract. Regulation reasonably related to a proper purpose and not arbitrary or capricious satisfies due process; a legislature may protect women first. \textit{Adkins} is overruled.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Liberty is ``liberty in a social organization,'' subject to reasonable regulation. The State may protect women's health and bargaining power, and sub-living wages force taxpayers to provide ``a subsidy for unconscionable employers.'' Sutherland insisted constitutional meaning does not change with economic events, called the law ``essentially arbitrary,'' and defended women's legal equality.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a gender-neutral minimum wage. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a women-only wage law today. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} protectionist hair-braider licensing.

\textbf{Critique.} Libertarians see judicial abdication; progressives praise the end of \textit{Lochner}; Cushman doubts the court-packing story.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The Constitution does not speak of freedom of contract. It speaks of liberty and prohibits the deprivation of liberty without due process of law. In prohibiting that deprivation, the Constitution does not recognize an absolute and uncontrollable liberty.'' --- Chief Justice Hughes, 300 U.S.\ at 391.
