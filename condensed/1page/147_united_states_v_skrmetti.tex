\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{United States v.\ Skrmetti}, 605 U.S.\ 495 (2025)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} A ban on puberty blockers and hormones for minors' gender dysphoria gets rational basis review (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10776831/united-states-v-skrmetti/}{CourtListener}).

\textbf{Facts.} Tennessee's SB1 bars puberty blockers and hormones for minors to treat gender dysphoria, while allowing them for congenital defects, precocious puberty, disease, or injury. The legislature cited risks of sterility and regret and European restrictions. Transgender adolescents, parents, and a doctor sued; the United States intervened, then switched sides in 2025.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court enjoined the ban; the Sixth Circuit reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 6--3: Roberts for the Court; Thomas, Barrett, and Alito concurred; Sotomayor and Kagan dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} SB1 classifies by age and medical use, not sex or transgender status, and rationally protects minors amid medical uncertainty.

\textbf{Reasoning.} A treatment is drug plus diagnosis, and SB1 bars nothing for one sex that it permits the other; under \textit{Geduldig} it does not single out transgender status, and \textit{Bostock}'s test fails. Citing the Cass Review, the Court gave the State latitude amid medical uncertainty. Barrett and Alito would hold transgender status not a suspect class; Thomas warned against deferring to ``self-described experts.'' Sotomayor saw a facial sex classification.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a Medicaid exclusion. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a sex-based dress code. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} birth-certificate sex markers.

\textbf{Critique.} Defenders praise leaving pediatric medicine to voters; Kate Redburn faults the ``double deference'' of an undefined medical context.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In the medical context, the mere use of sex-based language does not sweep a statute within the reach of heightened scrutiny.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, for the Court.
