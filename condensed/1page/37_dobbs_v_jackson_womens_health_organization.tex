\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization}, 597 U.S. 215 (2022)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} \textit{Roe} and \textit{Casey} overruled: no constitutional right to abortion (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6481357/dobbs-v-jackson-womens-health-organization/}{CourtListener}).

\textbf{Facts.} Mississippi banned abortion after 15 weeks, with exceptions only for medical emergency or severe fetal abnormality. The State's only abortion clinic sued, relying on \textit{Roe} and \textit{Casey}'s bar on pre-viability bans.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court enjoined the Act; the Fifth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} 6--3 to uphold the ban, 5--4 to overrule: Alito for the Court; Thomas and Kavanaugh concurred; Roberts concurred in the judgment; Breyer, Sotomayor, and Kagan jointly dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Unenumerated rights must be ``deeply rooted in this Nation's history and tradition''; abortion is not. Abortion laws get rational-basis review, which the ban satisfies.

\textbf{Reasoning.} By 1868 three-quarters of the States banned abortion at all stages; abortion is ``critically different'' from other privacy rights because it destroys ``fetal life''; \textit{stare decisis} factors favored overruling. Thomas would revisit all substantive due process; Kavanaugh called the Constitution ``neither pro-life nor pro-choice''; Roberts would have upheld the ban without overruling \textit{Roe}. The dissent said the ``1868 test'' consigns women to ``second-class citizenship.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a total ban survives. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a ban on interstate abortion travel. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} laws protecting IVF embryos.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics say the history test entrenches women's exclusion and cannot coherently spare \textit{Griswold} or \textit{Obergefell}; some originalists reject substantive due process altogether.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``We hold that \textit{Roe} and \textit{Casey} must be overruled. The Constitution makes no reference to abortion, and no such right is implicitly protected by any constitutional provision, including the one on which the defenders of \textit{Roe} and \textit{Casey} now chiefly rely---the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.'' --- Justice Alito, 597 U.S. 215.
