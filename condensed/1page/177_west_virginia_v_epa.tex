\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{West Virginia v.\ EPA}, 597 U.S.\ 697 (2022)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The ``major questions doctrine,'' named: no clear authority in \S~111(d) for the Clean Power Plan's shift away from coal (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/597/20-1530/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} EPA had always set \S~111(d) limits by on-site technology. The 2015 Clean Power Plan instead required ``generation shifting'' from coal to gas and renewables, cutting coal's projected share of electricity from 38\% to 27\% by 2030.

\textbf{Procedural History.} After a Supreme Court stay, repeal, and a replacement rule, the D.C.\ Circuit vacated the repeal.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed 6--3: Roberts for the Court; Gorsuch, with Alito, concurred; Kagan, with Breyer and Sotomayor, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} In extraordinary cases of vast economic and political significance, an agency needs ``clear congressional authorization''; \S~111(d) provides none for generation shifting.

\textbf{Reasoning.} EPA claimed a ``transformative expansion'' of power in a statutory ``backwater,'' outside its expertise, adopting a cap-and-trade scheme Congress had ``considered and rejected.'' Gorsuch called the doctrine a clear-statement rule protecting Article~I; Kagan said the broad text covered the Plan.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} AI licensing under a 1974 lab-safety statute. \emph{Opposite-Side:} an on-site technology standard. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} SEC climate-risk disclosure rules.

\textbf{Critique.} Is the doctrine a judge-made canon or ordinary textualism, and is it a deregulatory ratchet? \textit{Learning Resources v.\ Trump} (2026) applied it to strike down a Republican President's tariffs.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``To convince us otherwise, something more than a merely plausible textual basis for the agency action is necessary. The agency instead must point to `clear congressional authorization' for the power it claims.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, 597 U.S.\ at 723.
