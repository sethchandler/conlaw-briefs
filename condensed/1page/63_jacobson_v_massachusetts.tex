\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Jacobson v. Massachusetts}, 197 U.S. 11 (1905)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Compulsory adult vaccination during a smallpox epidemic is a valid exercise of the police power (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/197/11/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} In 1902 the Cambridge Board of Health ordered all inhabitants vaccinated, with a \$5 fine for refusing adults. Pastor Henning Jacobson refused, citing past reactions, but offered no proof he was then unfit.

\textbf{Procedural History.} His evidence against vaccination was excluded; he was convicted and fined, and the Supreme Judicial Court affirmed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed 7--2: Harlan for the Court; Brewer and Peckham dissented without opinion.

\textbf{Holding.} A State may require vaccination during an outbreak; courts intervene only if a health law has ``no real or substantial relation'' to its object or plainly invades fundamental rights.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Liberty is ``liberty regulated by law''; a community may defend itself against epidemic disease, as quarantine and conscription show. Choosing among medical theories is for the legislature. But a law ``arbitrary and oppressive'' as applied, such as forcing vaccination on someone it would likely kill, would be struck down.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} school immunization mandates (\textit{Zucht}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} capping worship while sparing secular venues (\textit{Cuomo}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} indefinite booster fines for a mild endemic virus.

\textbf{Critique.} A modest \$5-fine precedent was inflated into an emergency super-precedent, and \textit{Buck v. Bell} relied on it.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``But the liberty secured by the Constitution of the United States to every person within its jurisdiction does not import an absolute right in each person to be, at all times and in all circumstances, wholly freed from restraint. There are manifold restraints to which every person is necessarily subject for the common good.'' --- Justice Harlan, 197 U.S. at 26.
