\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Green v. County School Board of New Kent County}, 391 U.S. 430 (1968)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} A ``freedom-of-choice'' plan that leaves a formerly segregated system racially identifiable fails the duty to desegregate when zoning would work (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/391/430/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Rural, residentially integrated New Kent County, Virginia, ran one white and one Black school. Its 1965 freedom-of-choice plan drew no white students to Watkins in three years, and 85\% of Black students stayed there.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court approved the plan; the en banc Fourth Circuit affirmed that approval.

\textbf{Votes.} Vacated and remanded, 9--0 (Brennan for the Court).

\textbf{Holding.} A board that ran a state-compelled dual system must convert to a unitary system across students, faculty, staff, transportation, activities, and facilities, with a plan that works now. Freedom of choice is not invalid per se but fails when it does not dismantle the dual system and better alternatives exist.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Opening the doors ``merely begins, not ends, our inquiry.'' After eleven years of delay, ``[t]he time for mere `deliberate speed' has run out.'' Freedom of choice is only a means; where zoning or splitting schools by grade would integrate at once, a plan leaving 85\% of Black children at Watkins shifted the burden from the Board to Black families. There were no separate opinions.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} integrating zones undercut by free transfers. \emph{Opposite-Side:} imbalance in a district never segregated by law. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a magnet plan producing partial integration.

\textbf{Critique.} Faulted for mandating ``compulsory integration''; praised for giving \textit{Brown} teeth, though later confined.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``School boards such as the respondent then operating state-compelled dual systems were nevertheless clearly charged with the affirmative duty to take whatever steps might be necessary to convert to a unitary system in which racial discrimination would be eliminated root and branch.'' --- Justice Brennan, 391 U.S. at 437--38.
