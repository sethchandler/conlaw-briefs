\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Chisholm v. Georgia}, 2 U.S. (2 Dall.) 419 (1793)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The case the Eleventh Amendment overturned: States suable by citizens of other States (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/2/419/}{2 U.S. 419}).

\textbf{Facts.} Georgia's commissioners bought war supplies in 1777 from South Carolina merchant Robert Farquhar and never paid. His executor, Alexander Chisholm, sued Georgia.

\textbf{Procedural History.} After a circuit court dismissed, Chisholm sued in \emph{assumpsit} in the Supreme Court's original jurisdiction; Georgia refused to appear.

\textbf{Votes.} 4--1 in seriatim opinions by Jay, Cushing, Wilson, and Blair; Iredell dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Article III's grant over controversies between a State and citizens of another State covers States as defendants, and Georgia had no sovereign immunity; default judgment would enter unless it appeared.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The text draws no plaintiff-only line, and sovereignty lies with the people, so a State, like a merchant, must answer for its contracts. Iredell found no common-law writ against a sovereign and insisted that each State remains sovereign as to reserved powers. The Eleventh Amendment (1795) overturned the decision.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a State suing as plaintiff. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a federal damages suit against a State on an unpaid contract, now barred. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a hospital authority as a possible arm of the State.

\textbf{Critique.} Modern immunity rests on structural postulates from Iredell's dissent rather than the text the majority read literally; some argue the Amendment repealed only diversity jurisdiction.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``No such ideas obtain here; at the Revolution, the sovereignty devolved on the people, and they are truly the sovereigns of the country, but they are sovereigns without subjects (unless the African slaves among us may be so called), and have none to govern but themselves; the citizens of America are equal as fellow citizens, and as joint tenants in the sovereignty.'' --- Chief Justice Jay, 2 U.S. at 471--472.
