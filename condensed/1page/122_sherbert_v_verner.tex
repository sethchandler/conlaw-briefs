\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Sherbert v.\ Verner}, 374 U.S.\ 398 (1963)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} No denial of unemployment benefits to a Sabbatarian absent a ``compelling state interest'' (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/374/398/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Adell Sherbert, a Seventh-day Adventist mill worker, was fired for refusing Saturday work and could find no five-day job. South Carolina denied benefits for refusing ``available suitable work'' without good cause.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The Commission denied benefits; the South Carolina courts affirmed.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed 7--2: Brennan for the Court; Douglas concurred; Stewart concurred in the result; Harlan, with White, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Forcing a choice between faith and benefits is a substantial burden that requires a compelling interest and no less restrictive alternative; extending benefits does not establish religion.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The choice works like a fine on Saturday worship, and benefits need not be a ``right.'' The State's fear of fraud was speculative. Stewart saw a clash with \textit{Braunfeld}; Harlan said the neutral rule disqualified everyone unavailable for personal reasons.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a pacifist who quits rather than build tanks (\textit{Thomas}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} a neutral peyote ban (\textit{Smith}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a worker refusing Saturdays for a secular duty.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics say \textit{Sherbert} privileges religion over secular conscience and invites judges to weigh religious claims; after \textit{Smith} curtailed it, Congress restored its test for federal action in RFRA.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The ruling forces her to choose between following the precepts of her religion and forfeiting benefits, on the one hand, and abandoning one of the precepts of her religion in order to accept work, on the other hand.'' --- Justice Brennan, 374 U.S.\ at 404.
