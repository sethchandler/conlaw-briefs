\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Allen v.\ Cooper}, 589 U.S.\ 248 (2020)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Blackbeard's shipwreck videos: Congress could not abrogate state immunity from copyright damages under Article~I or Section~5 (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/589/18-877/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Videographer Frederick Allen copyrighted his footage of the salvage of the \textit{Queen Anne's Revenge}. North Carolina, which owned the wreck, posted his videos online even after a \$15,000 settlement, and Allen sued for damages under the Copyright Remedy Clarification Act (CRCA).

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court upheld the CRCA under Section~5; the Fourth Circuit reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 9--0: Kagan for the Court; Thomas concurring in part; Breyer and Ginsburg concurring in the judgment.

\textbf{Holding.} The CRCA's abrogation is invalid. Article~I cannot abrogate state immunity, \textit{Katz} being limited to bankruptcy, and blanket abrogation fails Section~5's ``congruence and proportionality'' test.

\textbf{Reasoning.} \textit{Florida Prepaid}, which struck the CRCA's patent twin, controls absent a ``special justification.'' Infringement violates due process only if intentional or reckless and unremedied; the record showed twelve possible instances, mostly negligent. Thomas rejected the ``special justification'' standard; Breyer thought \textit{Seminole Tribe} wrong but binding.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a CRCA suit over a state university's copying. \emph{Opposite-Side:} an \textit{Ex parte Young} injunction suit. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a tailored statute reaching only willful, unremedied state infringement.

\textbf{Critique.} The Court counts the frequency of infringement rather than the share of covered conduct that is unconstitutional, and its reading of \textit{Katz} collapsed in \textit{Torres}.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``But everything in \textit{Katz} is about and limited to the Bankruptcy Clause; the opinion reflects what might be called bankruptcy exceptionalism.'' --- Justice Kagan.
