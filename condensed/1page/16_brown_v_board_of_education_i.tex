\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Brown v.\ Board of Education (Brown I)}, 347 U.S.\ 483 (1954)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} ``Separate but equal'' has no place in public education: state school segregation violates equal protection (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/347/483/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Four consolidated class actions by Black schoolchildren from Kansas, South Carolina, Virginia, and Delaware challenged laws requiring or permitting segregated public schools. In Topeka, the schools were found substantially equal, so the suit challenged segregation itself.

\textbf{Procedural History.} Three district courts upheld segregation under \textit{Plessy}; Delaware ordered admission. After reargument, the Court ruled in 1954 and set remedies for \textit{Brown II}.

\textbf{Votes.} 9--0: Chief Justice Warren for a unanimous Court.

\textbf{Holding.} Separating public school children solely by race violates the Equal Protection Clause even when tangible facilities are equal.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The 1868 history was inconclusive, and the Court ``cannot turn the clock back to 1868''; education must be judged by its present role. Building on \textit{Sweatt} and \textit{McLaurin}, Warren held that segregation itself generates a feeling of inferiority, citing social science in Footnote 11. Unanimity was deliberate, to deny the South a divided ruling.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} segregated but identical parks or buses. \emph{Opposite-Side:} imbalance from private housing choices alone. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} voluntary race-conscious integration plans.

\textbf{Critique.} Wechsler found no neutral principle; Charles Black and Derrick Bell answered from the realities of caste and politics.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Today, education is perhaps the most important function of state and local governments. Compulsory school attendance laws and the great expenditures for education both demonstrate our recognition of the importance of education to our democratic society.'' --- Chief Justice Warren, 347 U.S.\ at 493.
