\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Frontiero v. Richardson}, 411 U.S. 677 (1973)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The military could not presume wives dependent while making servicewomen prove their husbands' dependency; a plurality called sex a suspect classification (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/411/677/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Air Force Lieutenant Sharron Frontiero sought housing and medical benefits for her husband, a college student. Servicemen claimed wives automatically; servicewomen had to prove their husbands relied on them for over half their support. Joseph's veterans' benefits covered more than half his expenses, so the claim was denied.

\textbf{Procedural History.} A three-judge district court upheld the statutes 2--1 as a rational way to save administrative expense.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 8--1, with no majority opinion: Brennan for four; Stewart and Powell (with Burger and Blackmun) concurred in the judgment; Rehnquist dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The statutes violate the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause: under \textit{Reed v. Reed}, treating similarly situated men and women differently solely for administrative convenience is unconstitutional. Strict scrutiny drew only four votes; \textit{Craig v. Boren} (1976) later chose intermediate scrutiny.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Brennan treated sex as suspect, citing a history of romantic paternalism, women's political underrepresentation, immutability, and Congress's recent laws; the government had not even shown that automatic benefits saved money. Stewart relied on \textit{Reed}. Powell would not decide the standard while the Equal Rights Amendment was before the states. Rehnquist adopted the district court's opinion.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} widows' benefits paid automatically but widowers' only on proof. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a dependency test applied to all service members. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a widows-only tax exemption defended as remedial.

\textbf{Critique.} Originalists find no equal protection clause in the Fifth Amendment; feminists lament the one vote lost to an ERA never ratified.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``And what differentiates sex from such nonsuspect statuses as intelligence or physical disability, and aligns it with the recognized suspect criteria, is that the sex characteristic frequently bears no relation to ability to perform or contribute to society.'' --- Justice Brennan, plurality opinion, 411 U.S. at 686--87.
