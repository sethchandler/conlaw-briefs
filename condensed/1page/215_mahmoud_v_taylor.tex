\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Mahmoud v. Taylor}, 606 U.S. 522 (2025)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Religious parents are entitled to notice and opt-outs from LGBTQ+-inclusive elementary storybooks (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-297}{Cornell LII}).

\textbf{Facts.} A Maryland county added five LGBTQ+-inclusive storybooks to its K--5 curriculum, then rescinded notice and religious opt-outs while keeping opt-outs for sex education. Muslim, Catholic, and Orthodox parents sued.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court and a divided Fourth Circuit denied a preliminary injunction.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 6--3: Alito for the Court; Thomas concurring; Sotomayor, with Kagan and Jackson, dissenting.

\textbf{Holding.} Normative instruction that substantially interferes with children's religious development is a \textit{Yoder} burden triggering strict scrutiny regardless of neutrality; the policy failed it.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The ``unmistakably normative'' books, for children aged 5 to 11, threatened to undermine parents' teaching; existing opt-outs showed tailoring failed. Thomas warned against a ``playbook for evading the First Amendment''; Sotomayor said the Clause bars coercion, not ``exposure.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a workbook disparaging faith. \emph{Opposite-Side:} neutral high-school evolution. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a factual seventh-grade gender-identity lesson.

\textbf{Critique.} \textit{Mahmoud} erodes \textit{Smith} without overruling it and makes judges parse children's books.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``When the burden imposed is of the same character as that imposed in \textit{Yoder}, we need not ask whether the law at issue is neutral or generally applicable before proceeding to strict scrutiny.'' --- Justice Alito (slip op., at 36).
