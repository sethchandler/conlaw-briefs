\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority}, 469 U.S. 528 (1985)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Overruling \textit{National League of Cities}, the Court held that a city transit authority must obey the FLSA; the political process, not judicial enclaves, protects the States (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/469/528/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} After \textit{National League of Cities} (1976) immunized States ``in areas of traditional governmental functions,'' San Antonio's public transit system, heavily funded by federal grants, stopped following the FLSA's overtime rules.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The Labor Department found SAMTA covered; the district court twice held transit a traditional function. The Court ordered reargument on overruling \textit{National League of Cities}.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--4: Blackmun for the Court; Powell, Rehnquist, and O'Connor dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} \textit{National League of Cities} is overruled. State sovereignty is protected chiefly by the structure of the national political process, and the FLSA validly applies to SAMTA.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Lower courts could not consistently say which functions were traditional, and such line-drawing penalizes state experimentation. The Framers protected the States through the Senate, the Electoral College, and state control of House qualifications, and the States had won large grants and many exemptions. Powell protested the abandonment of precedent and the erosion of political safeguards; Rehnquist predicted the dissent would again command a majority; O'Connor invoked \textit{McCulloch} and the Tenth Amendment. \emph{Later:} \textit{New York} and \textit{Printz} bar commandeering, and \textit{Alden} bars private damages suits against States.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a general fleet-electrification rule binding public bus operators. \emph{Opposite-Side:} ordering state DMVs to enforce it. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} pay rules only for state judicial staff.

\textbf{Critique.} Nationalized politics may have weakened the political safeguards, and no process failure is defined.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``We therefore now reject, as unsound in principle and unworkable in practice, a rule of state immunity from federal regulation that turns on a judicial appraisal of whether a particular governmental function is `integral' or `traditional.'\,'' --- Justice Blackmun, 469 U.S. at 546.
