\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Tandon v. Newsom}, 593 U.S. 61 (2021)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Most-favored-nation free exercise: favoring any comparable secular activity triggers strict scrutiny (\href{https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/20a151_4g15.pdf}{Slip Opinion}).

\textbf{Facts.} California's COVID-19 rules capped all home gatherings at three households while letting salons, stores, theaters, and restaurants host more. Pastor Jeremy Wong and Karen Busch, who hosted home Bible studies and prayer for eight to twelve people, sued under the Free Exercise Clause. California announced changes during the litigation but kept the cap in force.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court and a divided Ninth Circuit refused relief, comparing religious home gatherings only to secular ones. The Supreme Court granted an injunction pending appeal.

\textbf{Votes.} 5--4 \textit{per curiam}; Roberts would have denied relief; Kagan, with Breyer and Sotomayor, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} A rule is not neutral and generally applicable if it treats any comparable secular activity better than religion; comparability turns on risk; the State must show secular precautions would not suffice; mid-litigation changes do not moot the case.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Equal treatment of secular home gatherings was ``no answer''; the Ninth Circuit wrongly rejected commercial comparators. Kagan replied that homes are riskier, citing longer interactions, poor ventilation, and less masking, so the right comparator was treated identically.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a no-beard rule with only medical exemptions. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a noise ordinance with no exemptions. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a medical-only vaccine mandate.

\textbf{Critique.} Supporters say \textit{Tandon} stops governments from subordinating religion to favored commerce; critics say the rule hollows out \textit{Smith}, since nearly every law has secular exceptions, and was made on the shadow docket.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``First, government regulations are not neutral and generally applicable, and therefore trigger strict scrutiny under the Free Exercise Clause, whenever they treat \textit{any} comparable secular activity more favorably than religious exercise.'' --- \textit{Per Curiam}, 593 U.S. at 62.
