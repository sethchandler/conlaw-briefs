\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{City of Boerne v. Flores}, 521 U.S. 507 (1997)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} RFRA falls as applied to the states: Section 5 legislation must show ``congruence and proportionality'' (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/521/507/}{521 U.S. 507}).

\textbf{Facts.} Boerne, Texas, denied Archbishop Flores a permit to enlarge St. Peter Catholic Church within a new historic district. Flores sued under RFRA, enacted after \textit{Employment Division v. Smith} to restore strict scrutiny of burdens on religion and applied to the states through Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court held RFRA invalid; the Fifth Circuit reversed; the Supreme Court reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} 6--3. Kennedy wrote for the Court; Stevens and Scalia concurred; O'Connor, Souter, and Breyer dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} RFRA exceeds Congress's Section 5 power as applied to state and local governments. Congress may enforce but not redefine constitutional rights, and its remedies must be proportional to a documented pattern of violations.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Section 5 grants only the power ``to enforce''; letting Congress change rights would circumvent Article V. Unlike the Voting Rights Act, RFRA rested on no record of religious bigotry and imposed sweeping, perpetual strict scrutiny. Stevens saw an Establishment Clause problem; O'Connor accepted the framework but would have overruled \textit{Smith}.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} ADA damages against states fail (\textit{Garrett}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} a targeted family-leave mandate survives (\textit{Hibbs}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} nationwide voter-ID limits without sunset or geographic targeting.

\textbf{Critique.} Post, Siegel, and McConnell call the test juricentric and ahistorical; federalism defenders see a needed guard against a congressional police power.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``There must be a congruence and proportionality between the injury to be prevented or remedied and the means adopted to that end. Lacking such a connection, legislation may become substantive in operation and effect.'' --- Justice Kennedy, Opinion of the Court, 521 U.S. at 520.
