\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Abrams v.\ United States}, 250 U.S.\ 616 (1919)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Leaflets urging a wartime munitions strike were punishable; Holmes's dissent founded modern free-speech law (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/250/616/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} In 1918 five Russian-born radicals threw leaflets from a New York window protesting U.S.\ intervention in Russia and urging munitions workers to a ``general strike.''

\textbf{Procedural History.} Convicted under the Espionage Act and sentenced to as much as twenty years.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 7--2: Clarke for the Court; Holmes, with Brandeis, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The leaflets were unprotected, and intent to hinder the war could be inferred from their foreseeable effects.

\textbf{Reasoning.} \textit{Schenck} settled the First Amendment issue, and men are accountable for the likely effects of their acts. Holmes answered that only a ``clear and imminent danger'' justifies punishment and that a ``silly leaflet by an unknown man'' posed none; \textit{Brandenburg} (1969) later adopted his imminence principle.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} speech coordinated with a terrorist group. \emph{Opposite-Side:} urging a strike next month. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a viral call to shut a plant ``right now.''

\textbf{Critique.} Holmes never acknowledged how far he had moved from \textit{Schenck}, and critics doubt that ``free trade in ideas'' fits an age of concentrated media power and viral disinformation.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``But when men have realized that time has upset many fighting faiths, they may come to believe even more than they believe the very foundations of their own conduct that the ultimate good desired is better reached by free trade in ideas -- that the best test of truth is the power of the thought to get itself accepted in the competition of the market, and that truth is the only ground upon which their wishes safely can be carried out.'' --- Justice Holmes, dissenting, 250 U.S.\ at 630.
