\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Trump v. Slaughter}, 609 U.S. 422 (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} \textit{Humphrey's Executor} is overruled; FTC Commissioners are removable at will (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/25-332/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} In March 2025 President Trump fired FTC Commissioner Rebecca Kelly Slaughter without the statutory cause of ``inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office,'' saying her service was inconsistent with his Administration's priorities. The FTC issues binding rules, adjudicates, and sues in federal court.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court reinstated her; the Court stayed that order and granted certiorari before judgment.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed 6--3: Roberts for the Court; Gorsuch concurred; Sotomayor, with Kagan and Jackson, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Principal officers exercising executive power must be removable at will; the FTC's protections are void and \textit{Humphrey's} is overruled.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The Framers chose a single, accountable President whose subordinates answer to him, as the Decision of 1789 and \textit{Myers} confirmed. \textit{Humphrey's} rested on a fictional view of the FTC that later cases eroded. Part III--B reserved advisory bodies, the Federal Reserve, and non-Article III judges. Gorsuch urged reviving nondelegation; Sotomayor decried a ``profound bait and switch.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} NLRB and FERC members become removable. \emph{Opposite-Side:} Federal Reserve Governors (\textit{Cook}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} Tax Court judges.

\textbf{Critique.} Aaron Nielson warns that ``severability stacking'' gives the President a windfall of delegated power; others predict purges of expert commissions at each transition.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``When an agency `executes' a congressional mandate against private parties, it exercises executive power---no ifs, ands, or quasis about it.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts.
