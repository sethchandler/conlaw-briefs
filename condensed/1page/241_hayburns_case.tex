\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Hayburn's Case}, 2 U.S. (2 Dall.) 409 (1792)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Justices on circuit refused pension duties subject to executive and congressional revision: courts do only judicial work, and their judgments are final (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/2/409/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} The Invalid Pensions Act of 1792 had circuit courts determine disabled veterans' eligibility and certify findings to the Secretary of War, who could withhold a name on ``cause to suspect imposition or mistake''; Congress could also revise awards.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The Pennsylvania Circuit refused William Hayburn's petition; Attorney General Randolph sought mandamus, and Congress mooted the case in 1793.

\textbf{Votes.} No merits judgment: the Court split 3--3 on Randolph's \textit{ex officio} motion and never ruled on his renewed one. Five Justices wrote circuit letters.

\textbf{Holding.} Per the letters, Congress may not assign Article III courts non-judicial duties or subject their judgments to executive or legislative revision.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The three branches are distinct and independent, and courts may receive only duties ``properly judicial'' and performed ``in a judicial manner.'' Revision would make judges subordinate advisers to the political branches. New York's judges served as commissioners out of court; Pennsylvania's refused; North Carolina's doubted the practice. \emph{Later:} \textit{Muskrat} (1911), \textit{Mistretta} (1989), and \textit{Plaut} (1995) built on these letters.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} an executive veto of court awards. \emph{Opposite-Side:} judges serving voluntarily on a commission. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} judgments paid only if Congress appropriates.

\textbf{Critique.} The judges condemned advisory opinions in unsolicited letters to the President; the commissioner device elevates form over independence; and, as Maeva Marcus and Robert Teir note, a reporter's footnote of lower-court letters became Article III canon.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``That by the Constitution of the United States, the government thereof is divided into three distinct and independent branches, and that it is the duty of each to abstain from, and to oppose, encroachments on either. That neither the Legislative nor the Executive branches can constitutionally assign to the Judicial any duties but such as are properly judicial, and to be performed in a judicial manner.'' --- New York Circuit Court, 2 U.S. (2 Dall.) at 410 n.*.
