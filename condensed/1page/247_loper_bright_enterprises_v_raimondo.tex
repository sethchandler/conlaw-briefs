\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo}, 603 U.S. 369 (2024)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} \textit{Chevron} is overruled; courts decide statutory authority independently (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-451}{Cornell LII}).

\textbf{Facts.} A 2020 fisheries rule made Atlantic herring fishermen pay for onboard observers; the statute authorized industry funding for three other groups but was silent on herring.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The D.C. and First Circuits upheld the rule under \textit{Chevron}; certiorari was limited to \textit{Chevron}'s fate.

\textbf{Votes.} Vacated and remanded, 6--3 (6--2 in \textit{Loper Bright}); Roberts for the Court; Thomas and Gorsuch concurring; Kagan dissenting.

\textbf{Holding.} Courts must exercise independent judgment on whether an agency acted within its authority; \textit{Skidmore} respect and express delegations survive, and prior \textit{Chevron} holdings retain stare decisis effect.

\textbf{Reasoning.} APA \S~706 directs courts to ``decide all relevant questions of law,'' and since \textit{Marbury} courts never deferred on law. Ambiguity is not delegation, statutes have a ``single, best meaning,'' and \textit{Chevron} proved unworkable. \emph{Separately:} Thomas found a separation-of-powers violation; Gorsuch placed ``a tombstone on \textit{Chevron}''; Kagan decried ``a rule of judicial hubris.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} whether a 1950 chemical is ``emerging.'' \emph{Opposite-Side:} a threshold set under a ``reasonable and necessary'' delegation. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} an agency reversing a rule upheld under \textit{Chevron}.

\textbf{Critique.} The ``single best meaning'' may be a fiction that shifts policy to judges.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``\textit{Chevron} is overruled. Courts must exercise their independent judgment in deciding whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority, as the APA requires. \ldots{}'' Rather, ``courts need not and under the APA may not defer to an agency interpretation of the law simply because a statute is ambiguous.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, 603 U.S. at 412--413.
