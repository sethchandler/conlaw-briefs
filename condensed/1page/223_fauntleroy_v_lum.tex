\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Fauntleroy v. Lum}, 210 U.S. 230 (1908)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} A sister-state judgment must be enforced despite errors of law; only want of jurisdiction may be shown (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/210/230/}{210 U.S. 230}).

\textbf{Facts.} Two Mississippians made a cotton-futures contract that Mississippi made a crime and declared unenforceable ``by any court.'' An arbitrator ruled against Lum, who was later served while visiting Missouri.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The claimant had dropped a Mississippi suit on the award once its illegality surfaced. A Missouri court excluded Lum's illegality evidence and entered judgment on the award; the assignee, Fauntleroy, sued on it in Mississippi, whose Supreme Court refused to enforce it.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed 5--4. Holmes wrote for the Court; White, with Harlan, McKenna, and Day, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The Mississippi statute set a rule of decision, not a limit on jurisdiction; because Missouri had jurisdiction, full faith and credit made its judgment conclusive in Mississippi.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Jurisdiction is power and merits are duty, so a mistaken judgment is erroneous, not void. Judgments get the same ``credit, validity, and effect'' everywhere; Lum's remedy was appeal in Missouri, and mistakes would be ``rare.'' White warned the ruling would ``obliterate all state lines'' by letting each State overthrow its sisters' criminal laws, reading the Clause as mere comity. \textit{Baker v. General Motors} (1998) later reaffirmed that the obligation is ``exacting.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a wager judgment enforced in the wager's home State. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a penal judgment (\textit{Huntington}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a private bounty judgment.

\textbf{Critique.} The rule rewards ``tag'' jurisdiction and forum-shopping, and modern culture-war federalism tests Holmes's prediction that conflicts would be ``rare.''

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``A judgment is conclusive as to all the \textit{media concludendi}, \ldots{} and it needs no authority to show that it cannot be impeached either in or out of the state by showing that it was based upon a mistake of law. Of course, a want of jurisdiction over either the person or the subject matter might be shown. \ldots{} But, as the jurisdiction of the Missouri court is not open to dispute, the judgment cannot be impeached in Mississippi even if it went upon a misapprehension of the Mississippi law.'' --- Justice Holmes, 210 U.S. at 237.
