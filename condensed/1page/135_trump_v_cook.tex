\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Trump v.\ Cook}, 609 U.S.\ 528 (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Fed Governor Lisa Cook kept her seat: valid for-cause protection, and no pretermination notice (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/25a312/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} After an FHFA referral accused Cook of pre-office mortgage fraud, President Trump removed her ``for cause'' by letter, with no notice of the evidence or chance to respond.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court enjoined the removal; the D.C.\ Circuit denied a stay.

\textbf{Votes.} Stay denied 5--4: Roberts for the Court; Kavanaugh and Jackson concurred; Thomas, Alito (with Gorsuch), and Barrett dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Section 242 requires pretermination notice and an opportunity to respond, so the removal was void. ``Cause'' is a reviewable ``substantial threshold,'' and the Fed's protection is consistent with Article~II.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Central-bank independence has deep historical roots; ``cause'' requires serious misconduct with a nexus to official duties, not pretext. Thomas would have held the protection unconstitutional; Alito and Barrett said the Court decided too much on the emergency docket.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a Governor fired overnight over rate votes. \emph{Opposite-Side:} an SEC Commissioner, removable under \textit{Slaughter}. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a renewed removal after full process.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics find the Fed carveout incoherent beside \textit{Trump v.\ Slaughter}, and others ask why the logic of independence does not protect other regulators.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``At minimum, Cook was entitled to some explanation of the evidence at issue, some avenue for a response, and a deadline by which a response would be due.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, slip op.\ at 24.
