\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{United States v. Vaello Madero}, 596 U.S. 159 (2022)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Puerto Rico, SSI, and rational basis under the Territory Clause (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/20-303}{596 U.S. 159}).

\textbf{Facts.} SSI is limited to residents of the States and D.C. After Jose Luis Vaello Madero moved from New York back to Puerto Rico, payments continued until the SSA sued to recover \$28,081.

\textbf{Procedural History.} He won on Fifth Amendment equal-protection grounds in the district court and the First Circuit.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 8--1. Kavanaugh wrote for the Court; Thomas and Gorsuch concurred; Sotomayor dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Congress need not extend SSI to Puerto Rico; the territory's general exemption from federal income taxes is a rational basis.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Following \textit{Califano v.\ Torres} and \textit{Harris v.\ Rosario}, Congress may weigh the general balance of benefits and burdens without exact accounting. Gorsuch urged overruling the \textit{Insular Cases}. Sotomayor said SSI recipients owe no income tax anywhere, making the line irrational.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} excluding territories from a new credit. \emph{Opposite-Side:} excluding a low-revenue State. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a payroll-tax-funded benefit.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics say rational basis here ignores individual fit and leaves Footnote~1 unexplained.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In devising tax and benefits programs, it is reasonable for Congress to take account of the general balance of benefits to and burdens on the residents of Puerto Rico. In doing so, Congress need not conduct a dollar-to-dollar comparison of how its tax and benefits programs apply in the States as compared to the Territories, either at the individual or collective level.'' --- Justice Kavanaugh, Opinion of the Court, 596 U.S. at 165--66.
