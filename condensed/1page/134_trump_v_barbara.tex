{\Large \textit{Trump v. Barbara}, 609 U.S. \_\_\_ (2026) (No. 25-365)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Birthright citizenship covers U.S.-born children of unlawfully or temporarily present parents (\href{https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/25-365}{Cornell LII}).

\textbf{Facts.} Executive Order No.~14160 denied citizenship to U.S.-born children of unlawfully or temporarily present mothers and noncitizen fathers. After \textit{Trump v.\ CASA} curtailed universal injunctions, the ACLU sued for a nationwide class of affected children.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The District of New Hampshire enjoined the Order class-wide; certiorari before judgment.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 6--3 (5--4 on the Constitution): Roberts for the Court; Jackson concurring; Kavanaugh concurring in the judgment; Thomas, Alito, and Gorsuch dissenting.

\textbf{Holding.} ``Subject to the jurisdiction'' means subject to territorial governing power, incorporating \textit{jus soli} with narrow exceptions; neither President nor Congress may narrow it.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Common law, the repudiation of \textit{Dred Scott}, and \textit{Wong Kim Ark} confirm the territorial rule; domicile theories are ``dramatically revisionist.'' Jackson stressed the Clause's universalist drafting. Kavanaugh rested on the statute and would let Congress add exceptions; Thomas required domicile; Alito warned of rewarding ``birth tourists.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a statute excluding children of undocumented parents. \emph{Opposite-Side:} ambassadors' children. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} births in American Samoa.

\textbf{Critique.} Originalists split over the meaning of ``jurisdiction,'' and progressive scholars warn that the guarantee held by a single vote.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The Citizenship Clause uses jurisdiction in its ordinary sense---referring to the power of the United States to govern those within its territory.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts.
