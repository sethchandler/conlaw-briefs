\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{SEC v. Jarkesy}, 603 U.S. 109 (2024)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} SEC penalties for securities fraud require a jury (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/603/22-859/}{603 U.S.\ 109}).

\textbf{Facts.} Dodd-Frank let the SEC seek civil penalties in court or in its own jury-free proceedings. It brought an in-house fraud case against George Jarkesy and Patriot28 and imposed a \$300,000 penalty, a cease-and-desist order, industry bars, and disgorgement.

\textbf{Procedural History.} A divided Fifth Circuit vacated the SEC's order on three constitutional grounds.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 6--3 (Roberts for the Court; Gorsuch concurring; Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} Claims ``legal in nature'' carry the jury right, and punitive penalties make that showing ``all but dispositive.'' Securities fraud resembles common-law fraud and is not a public right, so Congress may not send it to agency adjudication.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The penalties punish and deter rather than compensate, and securities fraud borrows common-law terms. The public rights exception covers historic categories like revenue and immigration; \textit{Atlas Roofing} involved duties ``unknown to the common law.'' Gorsuch tied the jury right to Article~III and due process. Sotomayor called the ruling a ``power grab'' threatening over 200 statutes. \textit{FCC v.\ AT\&T} (2026) later upheld FCC forfeitures collectible only after a jury trial.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} in-house commodities-fraud penalties. \emph{Opposite-Side:} immigration carrier fines. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} immediately enforceable EPA penalties.

\textbf{Critique.} The ``all but dispositive'' remedy test leaves \textit{Atlas Roofing} on thin footing and the public-rights line unclear; defenders praise a clear jury baseline.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In sum, the civil penalties in this case are designed to punish and deter, not to compensate. They are therefore `a type of remedy at common law that could only be enforced in courts of law.' '' --- Chief Justice Roberts, 603 U.S.\ at 125.
