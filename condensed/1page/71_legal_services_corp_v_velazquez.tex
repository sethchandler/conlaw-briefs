\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez}, 531 U.S. 533 (2001)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Funded legal aid lawyers cannot be gagged from challenging welfare laws: private advocacy, not government speech (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/531/533/}{531 U.S. 533}).

\textbf{Facts.} A 1996 rider barred Legal Services Corporation grantees from welfare-reform litigation, allowing individual benefit suits only if they did not ``challenge existing law.'' Lawyers could argue factual or statutory error but had to withdraw if a constitutional or preemption issue arose.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The Second Circuit struck and severed the suits-for-benefits proviso; the Supreme Court granted certiorari.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 5--4: Kennedy for the Court; Scalia dissented for four.

\textbf{Holding.} When a funding program facilitates private speech, Congress may not impose viewpoint-based conditions that distort the bar and courts by excluding validity arguments to insulate its laws from review.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Unlike the doctors in \textit{Rust v. Sullivan}, who transmitted the government's message, LSC lawyers speak for clients against the government. The gag created ``two tiers of cases,'' impaired judicial review, and left indigent clients without substitute counsel; Congress cannot relabel it a ``definition of its program.'' Scalia saw a symmetrical subsidy condition governed by \textit{Rust}.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a tenant-defense program barring challenges to housing statutes. \emph{Opposite-Side:} funded counselors barred from endorsing vaping. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} asylum clinics barred only from systemic challenges.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics say it confuses abridging speech with declining to subsidize it; supporters praise it for cabining \textit{Rust}, though its mix of tests left no clear rule.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Although the LSC program differs from the program at issue in \textit{Rosenberger} in that its purpose is not to `encourage a diversity of views,' the salient point is that, like the program in \textit{Rosenberger}, the LSC program was designed to facilitate private speech, not to promote a governmental message.'' --- Justice Kennedy, Opinion of the Court, 531 U.S. at 542.
