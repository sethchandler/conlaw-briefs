\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Hein v. Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc.}, 551 U.S. 587 (2007)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} No taxpayer standing to challenge White House faith-based conferences funded by general appropriations (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/551/587/}{551 U.S. 587}).

\textbf{Facts.} President Bush's faith-based offices, created by executive order, were funded from lump-sum appropriations. Taxpayers sued, alleging that conferences favoring religious groups violated the Establishment Clause.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court dismissed; the Seventh Circuit, per Judge Posner, reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--4: Alito's plurality with Roberts and Kennedy; Scalia and Thomas concurring in the judgment; Souter and three others dissenting.

\textbf{Holding.} \textit{Flast v.\ Cohen} covers only spending authorized or mandated by a specific congressional enactment, not discretionary executive spending.

\textbf{Reasoning.} \textit{Flast} is confined to its facts; extending it would make courts monitors of the Executive. Kennedy warned against judges as ``speech editors''; Scalia would overrule \textit{Flast}; Souter saw the same injury whichever branch spends.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} agency workshops from a general account. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a statute directing grants to houses of worship. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a committee-report earmark.

\textbf{Critique.} The line is unprincipled and leaves a loophole for executive religious promotion.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``It is a necessary concomitant of the doctrine of \textit{stare decisis} that a precedent is not always expanded to the limit of its logic. That was the approach that then-Justice Rehnquist took in his opinion for the Court in \textit{Valley Forge}, and it is the approach we take here. We do not extend \textit{Flast}, but we also do not overrule it. We leave \textit{Flast} as we found it.'' --- Justice Alito (plurality), 551 U.S. at 615.
