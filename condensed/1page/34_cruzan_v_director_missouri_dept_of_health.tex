\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Cruzan v.\ Director, Missouri Department of Health}, 497 U.S.\ 261 (1990)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} A State may demand ``clear and convincing evidence'' of a vegetative patient's wishes before family may withdraw a feeding tube (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/261/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} A 1983 car accident left Nancy Cruzan in a persistent vegetative state, sustained by a feeding tube. Her parents sought to end artificial nutrition; the state hospital refused without court approval.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The trial court authorized withdrawal; the Missouri Supreme Court reversed, 4--3, requiring clear and convincing evidence.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed 5--4: Rehnquist for the Court; O'Connor and Scalia concurred; Brennan (with Marshall and Blackmun) and Stevens dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} A competent person has a due process liberty interest in refusing treatment (assumed to include nutrition and hydration), but a State may require clear and convincing proof of an incompetent patient's wishes and need not defer to family judgment.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The State's interest in life and the irrevocability of an erroneous withdrawal justify placing the risk of error on those seeking it. O'Connor would honor designated proxies; Scalia said ``the federal courts have no business in this field''; the dissents called the right fundamental and the error two-sided.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a clear-proof rule for ventilator withdrawal. \emph{Opposite-Side:} force-feeding a competent, refusing patient. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a designated proxy without specific instructions.

\textbf{Critique.} Tube feeding is no neutral ``status quo,'' and the rule favors those who draft living wills.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The principle that a competent person has a constitutionally protected liberty interest in refusing unwanted medical treatment may be inferred from our prior decisions.'' --- Chief Justice Rehnquist, 497 U.S.\ at 278.
