{\Large \textit{Louisiana v. Callais}, 608 U.S. \_\_\_ (2026)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} A second majority-Black Louisiana district fell as a racial gerrymander because Section~2, now requiring a strong inference of intent, did not require it (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/608/24-109/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} After a court found Louisiana's map likely violated Section~2, the Legislature added District~6, a 250-mile diagonal drawn to protect Republican leaders. ``Non-African American'' voters led by Phillip Callais sued.

\textbf{Procedural History.} A three-judge court enjoined the map, 2--1; the Supreme Court ordered reargument.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 6--3: Alito for the Court; Thomas (with Gorsuch) concurring; Kagan (with Sotomayor and Jackson) dissenting.

\textbf{Holding.} Section~2 compliance can be compelling, but liability requires a strong inference of intentional discrimination, shown by a race-blind map meeting all the State's goals (including partisan ones), polarization controlling for party, and current evidence.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The Fifteenth Amendment bars only purposeful discrimination, so \textit{Boerne} confines Section~2; Alito ``updated'' \textit{Gingles} for a two-party South and \textit{Rucho}. Thomas would exclude districting from Section~2; Kagan said the Court restored the \textit{Bolden} intent test Congress rejected in 1982.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} cracking a Black district for a 6--0 partisan map. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a one-party city rejecting race-blind maps. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} racist emails alongside a partisan goal.

\textbf{Critique.} Defenders see color-blind equal protection; critics see the nullification of Section~2.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In short, \S~2 imposes liability only when the evidence supports a strong inference that the State intentionally drew its districts to afford minority voters less opportunity because of their race.'' --- Justice Alito (slip op., at 26).
