\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{United States v. Lopez}, 514 U.S. 549 (1995)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The school-zone gun case: the first modern limit on the commerce power (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/117927/united-states-v-lopez/}{514 U.S. 549}).

\textbf{Facts.} A San Antonio high-school senior brought a handgun to school and was charged under the Gun-Free School Zones Act of 1990, which had no findings about commerce and no jurisdictional element.

\textbf{Procedural History.} Convicted after a bench trial, Lopez won reversal in the Fifth Circuit; the Supreme Court granted certiorari.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 5--4. Rehnquist wrote for the Court; Kennedy and Thomas concurred; Stevens, Souter, Breyer, and Ginsburg dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Congress may regulate channels and instrumentalities of interstate commerce and activities that ``substantially affect'' it, but not local, noneconomic conduct linked to commerce only through attenuated reasoning.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The Act regulated no economic enterprise and was not part of a larger market scheme, unlike \textit{Wickard}; its ``costs of crime'' rationale had no limiting principle. Kennedy stressed political accountability; Thomas urged a return to the original meaning of ``commerce''; Breyer found a rational basis in education's economic effects.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a federal truancy crime. \emph{Opposite-Side:} home marijuana cultivation within a drug-market scheme (\textit{Raich}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} the 1996 Act with a jurisdictional hook.

\textbf{Critique.} Originalists call it a half-measure; progressives say it revived pre-1937 formalism.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``To uphold the Government's contentions here, we would have to pile inference upon inference in a manner that would bid fair to convert congressional authority under the Commerce Clause to a general police power of the sort retained by the States.'' --- Chief Justice Rehnquist, Opinion of the Court.
