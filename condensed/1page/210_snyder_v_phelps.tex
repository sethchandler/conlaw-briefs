\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Snyder v. Phelps}, 562 U.S. 443 (2011)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Peaceful picketing on public issues near a Marine's funeral is protected from tort liability (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/562/443/}{562 U.S. 443}).

\textbf{Facts.} Westboro Baptist Church members, following police directions, picketed on public land about 1,000 feet from Marine Lance Corporal Matthew Snyder's Maryland funeral with signs such as ``Thank God for Dead Soldiers.'' His father, Albert Snyder, saw only the tops of the signs.

\textbf{Procedural History.} Snyder sued in federal court in Maryland. A jury awarded him \$10.9 million (reduced to \$5 million) for IIED, intrusion upon seclusion, and civil conspiracy; the Fourth Circuit reversed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed 8--1. Roberts wrote for the Court; Breyer concurred; Alito dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The First Amendment bars tort liability for peaceful picketing on matters of public concern in a traditional public forum; the captive-audience doctrine does not apply.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Public-issue speech occupies the ``highest rung of the hierarchy of First Amendment values,'' judged by ``content, form, and context.'' The signs' ``overall thrust and dominant theme'' was public, Westboro had no prior grudge against the family, and it stood in ``the archetype of a traditional public forum.'' The ``outrageousness'' standard invites juries to punish unpopular views. Breyer stressed narrowness; Alito saw a ``vicious verbal assault'' on grieving private figures.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a distant protest at a police funeral. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a private feud dressed up with slogans. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} residential picketing plus doxxing.

\textbf{Critique.} As Alito noted, deliberately targeting a grieving stranger for publicity earns more protection than a private feud, and a few political slogans may shift the ``dominant theme'' enough to immunize harassment.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Speech is powerful. It can stir people to action, move them to tears of both joy and sorrow, and---as it did here---inflict great pain. On the facts before us, we cannot react to that pain by punishing the speaker. As a Nation we have chosen a different course---to protect even hurtful speech on public issues to ensure that we do not stifle public debate.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts, 562 U.S. at 460--461.
