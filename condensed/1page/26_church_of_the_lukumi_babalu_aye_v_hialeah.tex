\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah}, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The Santeria animal-sacrifice case: a ``religious gerrymander'' is neither neutral nor generally applicable and fails strict scrutiny (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/508/520/}{508 U.S. 520}).

\textbf{Facts.} After a Santeria church announced plans to open in Hialeah, an openly hostile council banned ritual animal ``sacrifice'' while exempting slaughterhouses, kosher slaughter, hunting, fishing, and pest control.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The District Court upheld the ordinances as serving compelling interests; the Eleventh Circuit affirmed \textit{per curiam}.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 9--0. Kennedy wrote for the Court (his motive section joined only by Stevens); Scalia, Souter, and Blackmun wrote concurrences.

\textbf{Holding.} A law that targets religious conduct, or pursues its goals only against religiously motivated conduct, is not neutral or generally applicable under \textit{Smith} and must satisfy strict scrutiny, which these ordinances failed.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Facial neutrality is only the minimum. The ordinances' definitions and exemptions made Santeria sacrifice virtually the only conduct banned, and they were badly underinclusive as to animal cruelty and public health, so they were neither narrowly tailored nor compelling. Scalia rejected inquiry into legislators' motives; Souter and Blackmun urged reconsidering \textit{Smith}.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a ban on ``ceremonial animal killing'' aimed at a mosque's Eid slaughter. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a uniform rule that all must seal animal waste. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} neutral enforcement by officials who disparage a baker's faith.

\textbf{Critique.} The Court never defined how much underinclusiveness defeats general applicability, a gap later fought over in \textit{Fulton} and \textit{Tandon}.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Official action that targets religious conduct for distinctive treatment cannot be shielded by mere compliance with the requirement of facial neutrality. The Free Exercise Clause protects against governmental hostility which is masked as well as overt.'' --- Justice Kennedy, Opinion of the Court, 508 U.S. at 534.
