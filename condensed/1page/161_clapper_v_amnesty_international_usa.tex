\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Clapper v.\ Amnesty International USA}, 568 U.S.\ 398 (2013)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} No standing to challenge FISA Section 702 surveillance that was not ``certainly impending'' (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/568/398/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Section 702 (2008) permits targeting non-U.S.\ persons abroad without individualized probable cause. U.S.\ lawyers, journalists, and human rights groups who talk with foreign contacts sued, citing curtailed communications and travel costs.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court found no standing; the Second Circuit reversed, finding an ``objectively reasonable likelihood'' of interception.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--4: Alito for the Court; Breyer, with Ginsburg, Sotomayor, and Kagan, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} Threatened injury must be certainly impending, and self-inflicted costs cannot create standing.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Respondents' theory rested on a five-link chain of government choices, FISC approval, and successful interception, speculating about independent actors; precautions bought ``for the price of a plane ticket'' cannot lower the bar, and a subjective chill is not enough. Breyer found interception highly likely and said ``certainty is not, and never has been, the touchstone of standing.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} credit monitoring after a breach that took no data. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a repeat election-law complaint (\textit{Driehaus}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} traffic on tapped cables.

\textbf{Critique.} Supporters praise Article III discipline; critics see a Catch-22 shielding secret surveillance from review, noting that the criminal-defendant notice the Court relied on was being withheld until October 2013.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``In other words, respondents cannot manufacture standing merely by inflicting harm on themselves based on their fears of hypothetical future harm that is not certainly impending.'' --- Justice Alito, 568 U.S.\ at 416.
