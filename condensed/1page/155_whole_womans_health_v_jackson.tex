\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Whole Woman's Health v. Jackson}, 595 U.S. 30 (2021)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Federal courts could not enjoin state judges, clerks, or the Attorney General to block Texas's private-enforcement abortion ban (\href{https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/21-463_3ebh.pdf}{Slip Op.}).

\textbf{Facts.} Texas's S.B. 8 banned abortion after about six weeks, barred official enforcement, and let any private person sue for at least \$10,000. Providers sued a judge, a clerk, the Attorney General, licensing officials, and an activist.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court denied dismissal; the Supreme Court took the case before judgment.

\textbf{Votes.} Gorsuch for the Court: 9--0 dismissing the judge and activist; 5--4 dismissing the clerk and Attorney General; 8--1 keeping licensing officials.

\textbf{Holding.} \textit{Ex parte Young} reaches only executive officials with authority to enforce the law; courts may not enjoin judges, clerks, officials without enforcement power, unnamed private persons, or ``laws themselves.''

\textbf{Reasoning.} Judges are not adverse to litigants, enjoining a state court's machinery offends dual sovereignty, clerks are ministerial, and federal defenses remain available in state court. \emph{Separately:} Thomas would dismiss the licensing officials too; Roberts would keep the clerk and Paxton, warning that S.B. 8 aims to nullify the Court's rulings; Sotomayor warned other States would copy the scheme.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} suing judges to block a gun-bounty law. \emph{Opposite-Side:} suing a commissioner who must revoke accreditation. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} a registrar who certifies compliance before suits.

\textbf{Critique.} Praised for equitable restraint; faulted for spurring ``vigilante federalism.''

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Consistent with historical practice, a federal court exercising its equitable authority may enjoin named defendants from taking specified unlawful actions. But under traditional equitable principles, no court may `lawfully enjoin the world at large,' \dots{} or purport to enjoin challenged `laws themselves.' '' --- Justice Gorsuch, Opinion of the Court.
