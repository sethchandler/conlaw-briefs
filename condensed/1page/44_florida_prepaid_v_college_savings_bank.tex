\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v.\ College Savings Bank}, 527 U.S.\ 627 (1999)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The Patent Remedy Act's abrogation of state immunity failed \textit{City of Boerne}'s ``congruence and proportionality'' test (\href{https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/118316/florida-prepaid-postsecondary-education-expense-board-v-college-savings/}{CourtListener}).

\textbf{Facts.} College Savings Bank alleged that Florida Prepaid, an arm of the State, willfully infringed its patent on tuition-guarantee CDs. The 1992 Patent Remedy Act declared that States ``shall not be immune'' from federal infringement suits.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court denied Florida's immunity motion; the Federal Circuit affirmed under \S~5.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--4: Rehnquist for the Court; Stevens, with Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} The Act is not appropriate \S~5 legislation: Congress identified no pattern of unremedied, intentional state infringement and did not tailor its remedy.

\textbf{Reasoning.} With Article~I foreclosed by \textit{Seminole Tribe}, only \S~5 remained. The record showed few state infringements; infringement denies due process only without adequate state remedies, and negligence is no deprivation; yet the Act reached all infringement by all States. Stevens answered that exclusive federal jurisdiction left no state remedy and that the Act scaled with actual infringement.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} copyright abrogation (\textit{Allen v.\ Cooper}). \emph{Opposite-Side:} FMLA family leave (\textit{Hibbs}). \emph{Fence-Sitter:} grants conditioned on waiver.

\textbf{Critique.} Critics call it a ``juricentric Constitution'' resting on nontextual immunity.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``We thus held that for Congress to invoke \S~5, it must identify conduct transgressing the Fourteenth Amendment's substantive provisions, and must tailor its legislative scheme to remedying or preventing such conduct.'' --- Chief Justice Rehnquist, 527 U.S.\ at 639.
