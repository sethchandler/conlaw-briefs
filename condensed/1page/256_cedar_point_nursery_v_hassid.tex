\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Cedar Point Nursery v.\ Hassid}, 594 U.S.\ 139 (2021)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} Union access to farms 120 days a year was a \emph{per se} taking of the right to exclude (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/594/20-107/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} A California regulation gave unions a ``right to take access'' to farms for up to three hours a day, 120 days a year. Two growers, Cedar Point Nursery and Fowler Packing, challenged it after organizers entered or were blocked.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The district court applied \textit{Penn Central} and dismissed; the Ninth Circuit affirmed.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed 6--3: Roberts for the Court; Kavanaugh concurred; Breyer, with Sotomayor and Kagan, dissented.

\textbf{Holding.} A regulation granting third parties a right to physically enter private property is a \emph{per se} taking, however intermittent, apart from isolated trespasses, background privileges, and inspection conditions.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Appropriations, unlike use restrictions, require compensation without balancing; the right to exclude is fundamental; duration affects only the amount owed. \textit{PruneYard} concerned property open to the public. Breyer: the rule regulates rather than appropriates, and only permanent occupations are \emph{per se} takings.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} wildlife surveys on fenced timberland. \emph{Opposite-Side:} food-safety inspections as a license condition. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} organizers on a supermarket's sidewalk.

\textbf{Critique.} The exceptions are fuzzy, the remedy's relation to \textit{Knick} is unclear, and \textit{PruneYard}'s survival is uncertain.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The essential question is not, as the Ninth Circuit seemed to think, whether the government action at issue comes garbed as a regulation (or statute, or ordinance, or miscellaneous decree). It is whether the government has physically taken property for itself or someone else---by whatever means---or has instead restricted a property owner's ability to use his own property.'' --- Chief Justice Roberts.
