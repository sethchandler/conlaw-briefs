\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Barron v.\ Mayor \& City Council of Baltimore}, 32 U.S.\ (7 Pet.) 243 (1833)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The Fifth Amendment's Takings Clause, and by extension the Bill of Rights, binds only the federal government, not the States (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/32/243/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Baltimore's street grading diverted streams that silted up John Barron's deep-water wharf, rendering it nearly worthless. The city paid nothing, and Barron claimed a taking without just compensation.

\textbf{Procedural History.} A county jury awarded Barron \$4,500; Maryland's high court reversed; Barron took a writ of error under Section 25 of the Judiciary Act of 1789.

\textbf{Votes.} Writ dismissed, 7--0 (Marshall, C.J., for a unanimous Court).

\textbf{Holding.} The Takings Clause, and by the opinion's reasoning the Bill of Rights generally, limits only the federal government; with no federal question, the Court lacked jurisdiction. Fourteenth Amendment incorporation later largely superseded the rule.

\textbf{Reasoning.} The people ordained the Constitution ``for their own government, and not for the government of the individual States,'' each of which had its own constitution. Article I, \S~9's general limits bind only Congress, while \S~10 binds the States in express ``No State shall'' terms; the Fifth Amendment lacks such words. Citizens could amend their own state constitutions, and the amendments answered fears of the national government, ``not against those of the local governments.''

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a State charging murder by information rather than indictment. \emph{Opposite-Side:} a city's \$1 condemnation award, now barred via the Fourteenth Amendment. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} state troops quartered in homes under an unincorporated Third Amendment.

\textbf{Critique.} Praised for disciplined intratextualism; faulted because state constitutional reform was illusory for those without political power.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``The powers they conferred on this government were to be exercised by itself, and the limitations on power, if expressed in general terms, are naturally, and we think necessarily, applicable to the government created by the instrument.'' --- Chief Justice Marshall, 32 U.S. at 247.
