\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{Home Building \& Loan Ass'n v. Blaisdell}, 290 U.S. 398 (1934)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The Contracts Clause yields to a State's reasonable, temporary, conditioned emergency use of its police power (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/290/398/}{Justia}).

\textbf{Facts.} Minnesota's 1933 Mortgage Moratorium Law let courts extend redemption periods after foreclosure, until no later than May 1, 1935, if mortgagors paid reasonable rental value. The Blaisdells' home had been foreclosed.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The trial court set rent at \$40 a month and extended redemption; the Minnesota Supreme Court affirmed.

\textbf{Votes.} Affirmed, 5--4 (Hughes for the Court; Sutherland, with three others, dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} The Contracts Clause must be harmonized with the State's reserved police power; temporary emergency relief serving a public end on reasonable conditions protecting the creditor is valid.

\textbf{Reasoning.} An emergency does not create power but may furnish the occasion to use it. The test is whether the law serves a legitimate end by reasonable and appropriate means. A genuine emergency, public purpose, tailored judicial relief, preserved debt and rent, and temporary operation saved the law, and the Constitution's ``great clauses'' are read in light of the Nation's ``whole experience.'' \emph{Separately:} Sutherland said the Clause was written precisely to forbid debtor relief in depressions.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} a temporary pandemic foreclosure pause conditioned on rent. \emph{Opposite-Side:} permanent, uncompensated rent forgiveness. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} repealing retirees' COLA.

\textbf{Critique.} Living constitutionalism versus a clause aimed at stay laws; emergency exceptions become routine.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} The Contracts Clause's prohibition ``is not an absolute one, and is not to be read with literal exactness, like a mathematical formula.'' --- Chief Justice Hughes, 290 U.S. at 428.
