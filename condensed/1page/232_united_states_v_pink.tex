\begin{center}
{\Large \textit{United States v. Pink}, 315 U.S. 203 (1942)\par}
\vspace{0.3em}
{\normalsize Case Brief --- One-Page Edition\par}
\end{center}

\textbf{Memory Jogger.} The Litvinov Assignment: executive agreements preempt state law (\href{https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/315/203/}{315 U.S.\ 203}).

\textbf{Facts.} New York liquidated the New York branch of a Russian insurer that Soviet decrees had nationalized and planned to pay its surplus to foreign creditors. When President Roosevelt recognized the Soviet Union in 1933, the Litvinov Assignment, made without the Senate, assigned Soviet claims to the United States.

\textbf{Procedural History.} The New York courts dismissed the United States' suit for the surplus.

\textbf{Votes.} Reversed, 5--2 (Douglas for the Court; Frankfurter concurring; Stone and Roberts dissenting).

\textbf{Holding.} The recognition power includes settling claims by sole executive agreement, which has the same supremacy over state law and policy as a treaty. The United States took the surplus ahead of foreign creditors without violating the Fifth Amendment.

\textbf{Reasoning.} Recognition and claims settlement were one bargain, and letting New York refuse enforcement would revive the friction the President had resolved. Frankfurter rejected domestic concepts like ``situs'' and ``comity'' in international disputes. Stone said recognition does not reach New York assets.

\textbf{Examples.} \emph{Same-Side:} assigning a foreign airline's U.S.\ assets after recognition. \emph{Opposite-Side:} an agreement overriding a federal statute. \emph{Fence-Sitter:} extinguishing American creditors' liens.

\textbf{Critique.} The Supremacy Clause omits executive agreements; \textit{Medell\'in} later narrowed \textit{Pink}.

\textbf{Key Quotation.} ``Power to remove such obstacles to full recognition as settlement of claims of our nationals ... certainly is a modest implied power of the President who is the `sole organ of the federal government in the field of international relations.'\,'' --- Justice Douglas, for the Court.
